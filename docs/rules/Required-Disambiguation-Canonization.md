# Required Disambiguation Canonization (RDC)

#ttrpg #rules #rdc #narrative #core

Details can remain undefined — until they matter. When you're asked to specify something and you answer, that answer becomes permanent canon. This is the **Thread of Fate**.

---

## The Core Principle

> **When the DM asks for a detail and you answer, that answer becomes canon.**

Things that were undefined become defined. Things that are defined cannot be changed.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE THREAD OF FATE                                 │
│                                                    │
│  UNDEFINED ──────► DM ASKS ──────► YOU ANSWER      │
│                                          │         │
│                                          ▼         │
│                                    NOW CANON       │
│                                    (permanent)     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Example: Module Placement

```
SESSION 1:
  Player: "I fire my Thermal Lance."
  GM: "It hits. Moving on..."
  
  (Location of Thermal Lance: UNDEFINED)

SESSION 3:
  GM: "The enemy targets your right arm. What's mounted there?"
  Player: "My Thermal Lance."
  GM: "Okay, it's damaged."
  
  (Location of Thermal Lance: NOW CANON — right arm)
  
SESSION 4:
  Player: "Wait, I want my Thermal Lance on my left arm now."
  GM: "No — you declared it was on your right arm. That's canon."
```

---

## Why RDC?

### 1. Reduces Upfront Bookkeeping

You don't need to specify every detail at character creation. Fill in the blanks as they become relevant.

### 2. Emergent Detail

The world and your character fill in as you play. Details emerge from gameplay rather than being front-loaded.

### 3. Player Agency

You get to choose in the moment — when it matters most. It's your call.

### 4. Prevents Retroactive Convenience

Once you've declared something, you can't un-declare it. No "actually it was always on my other arm."

### 5. Artificial Luck

Undefined details let players "get lucky" in narratively interesting ways — but only once.

## The Lucky Shot: RDC as Narrative Fortune

Here's the fun part: RDC lets players make advantageous choices *in the moment* — and that's a feature, not a bug.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE LUCKY SHOT                                     │
│ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                    │
│ GM: "A shot rings out! Critical hit to your        │
│      Walker's RIGHT SHOULDER mount!"               │
│                                                    │
│ GM: "Wait... where are your Shoulder Missiles?     │
│      The loaded ones that would explode if hit?"   │
│                                                    │
│ Player: (hasn't defined this yet)                  │
│ Player: "Oh, those are on my LEFT shoulder."       │
│                                                    │
│ GM: (smiles) "Well, it's your lucky day. Your      │
│      unloaded decoy launcher got damaged — needs   │
│      repairs, but you avoided catastrophe!"        │
│                                                    │
│ (LEFT SHOULDER = Missiles is now CANON)            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Why This Works

The player didn't cheat — they made a choice about something that was genuinely undefined. But now:

- **The missiles are locked to LEFT shoulder forever**
- **Next time the left shoulder gets hit?** Boom.
- **The player used their "luck" — it's spent**

This creates **dramatic tension**: every undefined detail is a potential save... but also a potential lock-in that might hurt later.

---

## The Trade-Off

Players who **over-specify early** lose this flexibility. Everything is already canon — no room to maneuver.

Players who **stay vague** keep their options open — but risk the GM asking at the *worst possible moment*.

> **GM:** "The explosion tears through the cockpit. Where exactly is your ejection system?"
> 
> **Player:** "...I never said."
> 
> **GM:** "Then you get to decide right now. Choose wisely."

It's **Schrödinger's Loadout**: undefined until observed, then permanent.

---

## When RDC Applies

RDC can be invoked for any detail that was never specified:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Physical placement** | "Where is your module mounted?" |
| **Appearance** | "What does your character look like?" |
| **Backstory** | "Where did you get that weapon?" |
| **Relationships** | "How do you know this NPC?" |
| **Equipment details** | "What color is your Walker?" |
| **Location specifics** | "Which pocket is the key in?" |

If you never said, and it matters now, you get to decide — once.

```
│                                                    │

```

Include the session number so everyone remembers when details became canon.

## GM Guidance

### When to Invoke RDC

- **When a detail suddenly matters** — "Where's that component?"
- **When specificity creates drama** — "Who gave you that weapon?"
- **When players are being too vague** — "I need you to commit to something."

### When NOT to Invoke RDC

- **Trivial details** — Don't canonize everything; let flavor stay flexible
- **Mid-combat gotchas** — Don't weaponize RDC against players unfairly
- **Already established fiction** — If it was clearly implied, don't demand re-specification

### The Spirit of RDC

RDC exists to create interesting moments, not to trap players. If a player forgot they said something three sessions ago, be generous. If a player is clearly trying to game the system, push back.

The goal is collaborative storytelling, not legal proceedings.

---

## Design Notes

### Why "Required Disambiguation Canonization"?

Because it sounds appropriately formal and slightly arcane — fitting for a game called Gryst.

- **Required** — The DM is asking; you must answer
- **Disambiguation** — Clarifying something that was vague
- **Canonization** — Making it permanent, official, true

### Inspiration

This concept draws from:
- **Schrödinger's thought experiment** — Undefined until observed
- **Improv "yes, and"** — Building on what's established
- **Blades in the Dark's flashbacks** — Retroactive establishment (but RDC is present-tense)
- **OSR rulings over rules** — Embrace undefined space

RDC gives players agency while creating natural dramatic pressure. Use it to make your game more interesting.
