# Gambits

#ttrpg #rules #core #gambit

## What is a Gambit?

A **Gambit** is the atomic unit of resolution in Gryst — the fundamental building block for **everything you can do**.

**If it has an uncertain outcome and matters to the story, it's a Gambit.**

- Firing your rifle? That's a Gambit (from your rifle's Module).
- Casting a spell? That's a Gambit (from your Arcanist skill or a Module).
- Picking a lock? That's a Gambit.
- Convincing the guard? That's a Gambit.
- Jumping a chasm? That's a Gambit.

**Gambits are everywhere.** They're not a small list of "core actions" — they're the entire mechanical vocabulary of the game.

---

## Where Do Gambits Come From?

Gambits come from three sources, in priority order:

### 1. Module-Provided Gambits (Primary)

Your **equipment** grants Gambits through its Modules. A rifle with a Conventional Firearm Mechanism Module grants the "Fire Conventional Single Round" Gambit. A plasma rifle has different Modules with different Gambits.

**This is the primary source of Gambits.** Your gear defines what you can do.

### 2. Skill-Provided Gambits

Your **Skills** grant Gambits as you level up. A Gunslinger learns "Fan the Hammer." An Arcanist learns "Arcane Bolt."

These are inherent capabilities — you have them even without specific equipment.

### 3. Improvised & Fallback Gambits

When you try something that doesn't have a Module or Skill Gambit, you can **improvise**. The DM and player negotiate the outcomes together.

**Core Fallback Gambits** exist for truly generic situations (generic attack, generic defense), but these are last resorts, not the default.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GAMBIT PRIORITY                                    │
│                                                    │
│ "I shoot the cultist"                              │
│                                                    │
│ 1. Do I have a Module Gambit? → Use "Fire Single   │
│    Round" from my rifle's mechanism                │
│                                                    │
│ 2. Do I have a Skill Gambit? → Maybe "Trick Shot"  │
│    from Gunslinger if I'm doing something fancy    │
│                                                    │
│ 3. Neither? → Use Generic Attack (fallback)        │
│                                                    │
│ The goal: almost never use #3                      │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Pre-Authored Outcomes

**Gambits from Modules and Skills come with pre-authored outcomes.** You don't improvise what "Triumphus" means for your rifle shot — the "Fire Conventional Single Round" Gambit already defines it:

- **Triumphus:** Precision hit — full damage + choose: vital hit (+50%) OR target staggered
- **Successus:** Clean hit — deal weapon damage
- **Clades:** Miss — no damage, expend ammunition
- **Calamitas:** Malfunction — no damage, weapon jams

The outcomes are **designed as part of the Gambit**, not made up on the spot. This makes Gambits feel distinct and gives players predictable (but interesting) success/failure states.

**The DM can still modify outcomes** for narrative reasons, but the pre-authored outcomes are the baseline. See [[Core-Mechanic#Gambit Resolution Dice-Agnostic|Core Mechanic]] for how outcome quality is sacred.

---

## Gambit Outcomes

Every Gambit has four possible outcomes:

| Outcome | Latin | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Critical Success** | *Triumphus* | Exceptional result, bonus effects |
| **Success** | *Successus* | You achieve what you wanted |
| **Failure** | *Clades* | You don't achieve it, complications may arise |
| **Critical Failure** | *Calamitas* | Disaster, significant consequences |

## Declaring a Gambit

When introducing a Gambit, define:

1. **Intent:** What are you trying to accomplish?
2. **Approach:** How are you doing it?
3. **Stakes:** What's at risk?

The table (or rules) then determines:
- The **dice pool** needed
- The **ranges** for each outcome

### Example Gambit Declaration

> **"I want to leap across the chasm and grab the artifact before the cultist."**
> 
> - **Intent:** Get the artifact first
> - **Approach:** Athletic leap
> - **Stakes:** Fall into the chasm / cultist gets it
>
> **Triumphus:** Grab it AND knock the cultist back  
> **Successus:** Grab the artifact  
> **Clades:** Miss the grab, both scrambling for it  
> **Calamitas:** Fall into the chasm

## Improvised Gambits

When no authored Gambit covers the situation, players can **propose improvised Gambits** for:

- Creative solutions the game didn't anticipate
- Dramatic one-off moments
- Using the environment in unexpected ways
- Social maneuvers without a Social skill

The DM and player **negotiate the four outcomes together**, then determine appropriate dice.

### Improvisation Is Secondary

The system provides **extensive pre-authored Gambits** through Modules and Skills so you rarely need to improvise. When you do:

1. Check if a Module or Skill Gambit could apply (even loosely)
2. If nothing fits, propose an improvised Gambit
3. DM and player agree on the four outcomes
4. Play proceeds

### GM Authority

**The GM has full power over how Gambits are structured and resolved.** Use this power wisely.

The rules aim to cover common actions with authored Gambits for:
- Hero combat (personal scale)
- Walker (mecha) combat
- Heavenstrider (flyer) combat
- Social encounters
- Exploration and hazards

But **anyone can propose a Gambit** if the DM agrees it's fair and interesting.

### The Gryst Principle

> **ANYTHING IS POSSIBLE.** Gryst is a wacky reality. Instead of "you can't do that," stack the odds against the player if you must! Life is unpredictable.

- Mundane actions can suddenly require a Gambit if drama demands it
- Gambits can appear as unexpected boons
- Use sparingly so rolls don't become tedious

The key: **if failure doesn't matter, don't roll.** Gambits are for moments with meaningful stakes.

---

## Design Notes

- Four-tier system (not binary pass/fail) creates narrative texture
- Player-declared Gambits encourage creative problem-solving
- Stakes should always be meaningful — if failure doesn't matter, don't roll
