# The Consensus

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #consensus #storming

> *"Order from disorder. That is the way of the storm tamed."*

## Overview

The **Consensus** was — and in fractured form, still is — a monolithic state that governed most of the known universe. Originally centered on **Storming Gryst**, it expanded to become a rainbow entity comprising everything and anything. Its philosophy is simple: **bring order to the disordered**, much like the ancient founders tamed the storm and harnessed its shock.

## The Philosophy of Order

The Consensus arose from a people who **tamed the storm**. Storming Gryst — wild, chaotic, crackling with destructive potential — was harnessed and made to serve. Lightning became power. Thunder became industry. The shock of the storm accelerated their technology beyond all others.

From this origin came their guiding philosophy: **disorder can be ordered**. Chaos can be systematized. The wild can be made useful.

This philosophy extended beyond Storming Gryst. As the Consensus grew, it absorbed peoples of all Gryst affinities, becoming a **rainbow entity** — no longer defined by a single element, but by the principle of bringing order to all things.

---

## The Cryst Currency

The Consensus controls the only mints that produce **refined crystal Gryst** — the universal currency accepted on every Consensus world. This economic monopoly is one of the pillars of their power.

**Only Consensus mints** can produce this currency. Counterfeiting is nearly impossible given the Gryst refinement required. Control of money means control of trade, which means control of civilization.

---

## Electronics and Arcane Artifacts

The Consensus is the **predominant producer of electronics** — arcane artifacts empowered by the shock of the storm. Storming Gryst flows through their devices, powering everything from communication systems to weapons to computational engines.

If you use advanced technology in the Consensus sphere, chances are it bears Consensus manufacture or Consensus-derived principles.

## Government: The Council of Elder Magistrates

The Consensus is not *led* — it is **"guided"** by a council of **Elder Magistrates**.

### The 46

There are **46 Elder Magistrates** at any time. This number is symbolic: it represents the supposed number of years it took the legendary founder to establish the Consensus. Though no one is quite certain what this means anymore, for the Consensus was ever-growing.

### Selection and Tenure

- Elder Magistrates are **elected by existing Elder Magistrates**
- The office is held **for life**
- However, a Magistrate can be **removed by majority vote** of the council

This creates a self-perpetuating oligarchy tempered by internal accountability. New blood enters only when the council permits.

### Magistrates

Below the Elder Magistrates are ordinary **Magistrates** — administrators, judges, governors. The bureaucracy runs deep, and Magistrates occupy every level of it.

## Meritocracy and Governance

The Consensus is **nominally a meritocracy**, and it runs decently well. It's not a complete mess. Competence is rewarded. Corruption exists but is not systemic.

### Voluntary Membership (Mostly)

Most Consensus planets **joined voluntarily**. The benefits of membership — trade access, currency, technology, protection — outweigh independence for most civilizations.

However, the Consensus is **not adverse to subjugation by force** if they really want a planet in the fold. They simply don't feel the urge to assimilate *every* Grystborn world. Some planets aren't worth the trouble.

### No State Religion

There is **no centralized state religion**. Most Consensus planets retain their original faiths, though heavy **syncretism** occurs as cultures intermingle. A world might worship its old gods under new names, or blend three religions into one.

The Consensus cares about order, not theology.

### Racism (Or Lack Thereof)

The Consensus as an institution has **little racism** between Grystborn species. However, this does not mean every Consensus planet shares these views. Local prejudices persist despite official tolerance.

## The Special Relationship: Forgelords

The Consensus and [The Forgelords](worldbuilding/factions/The-Forgelords.md) share a **special relationship**. Together, they held dominion over most Grystborn space.

**The Consensus provided:**
- Markets and currency
- Political legitimacy
- Bureaucratic systems
- Electronics and storm-tech

**The Forgelords provided:**
- Industrial output
- Weapons and machinery
- Raw materials
- Manufacturing capacity

This partnership made both factions indispensable to the other — and to the wider universe.

## The Fracturing

**The Consensus has been fractured.**

The exact causes remain contested, debated, and obscured by propaganda. What is known:

- The unified Consensus no longer exists
- Multiple successor states claim legitimacy
- Many former worlds have diverged

### The Novus Consensus

The **Novus Consensus** exists as an attempt to restore the old ideals. It is:

- More **militaristic** than its predecessor
- Still striving to maintain the old way
- Claiming to be the legitimate continuation

Whether the Novus Consensus can reunify the fractured whole remains to be seen.

### Divergent Worlds

Many worlds once bound to the Consensus have now **diverged**. They still maintain Consensus influence — the currency, the technology, the bureaucratic systems — but are free to tread their own path.

These divergent worlds offer **blank canvases** for stories: coherent universe, shared history, but freedom to develop unique cultures and conflicts.

## Design Notes

### Why the Fracturing?

The fractured Consensus provides:

- **Narrative freedom** — No monolithic authority constraining stories
- **Political complexity** — Multiple factions with legitimate claims
- **Adventure hooks** — Power vacuums, succession crises, border conflicts
- **Player agency** — Characters can matter in a destabilized universe

### The 46 Mystery

The symbolic 46 Elder Magistrates invites questions:
- Who was the founder?
- What did those 46 years involve?
- Is the number actually meaningful, or just tradition?

### Blank Canvas Worlds

Divergent worlds are explicitly designed as **story spaces**. They have Consensus DNA (shared tech, currency, history) but freedom to be anything the campaign needs.

### Religion and Society Blur

> "No religion is a monolith, no society is a monolith, and sometimes the line between a religion and a society even blurs."

This is especially true in Gryst. Groups might be religious orders, political factions, cultural movements, or all three at once. Categories are suggestions, not constraints.
