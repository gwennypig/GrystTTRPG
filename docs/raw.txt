## What is Gryst?

Gryst is a TTRPG built around dynamic initiative and collaborative action. Instead of rolling for turn order once and cycling through, players **race** to earn their turns through a **Momentum** system — and when it's time to act, everyone can jump in through **Add-Ons** to build a stack of gambits that resolve as one cinematic moment.

## Core Concepts

### Momentum
Every character has a Momentum tracker (d20 counter). Each "tick," everyone adds their Momentum Gain simultaneously. First to hit 20 becomes the **Pivotal Character** and kicks off a resolution phase.

### Parties & Cooperation
Combat organizes into **Parties** with shared goals. **Cooperation** lets you freely Add-On to partymates. To break into an enemy's stack, you need a **Hijack** action — which opens the door for your whole party (at a cost).

### The Stack
When the Pivotal Character acts, allies can **Add-On** — spending momentum to slot actions before or after in a queue. **Hijacks** couple with their targets. The whole stack resolves as one narrative beat.

### Gambits
Every action is a **Gambit** with four possible outcomes:
- **Triumphus** — Critical success
- **Successus** — Success  
- **Clades** — Failure
- **Calamitas** — Critical failure

### Modules & Loculi
Everything that *does something* is built from **Modules** (which grant gambits, stats, perks). Modules live in **Loculi** (containers with Mass capacity). Three values define equipment: **Mass** (size), **Complexitās** (cost), **Magnitūdō** (power scaling).

### Magic = Tech
There's no distinction between magic and technology in Gryst. Both are ways of understanding and manipulating **Gryst** — the esoteric fabric of reality. A fireball and a plasma rifle are mechanically identical.

# Advancement

#ttrpg #rules #progression #sparkle #skills

## Philosophy

Advancement in Gryst is about **expanding options** more than raw power. A veteran character has more Gambits, more flexibility, and deeper specialization — but a starting character is already competent and dangerous.

**Core principles:**
- **Horizontal growth** — more tools, not bigger numbers
- **Identity sharpening** — AP forces you to define who you are
- **Multiclass freedom** — branch into new Skills anytime
- **No level cap** — but diminishing returns at high levels

---

## Advancement Triggers

Characters advance through **Milestone Rewards**, granted by the GM at story beats:

| Trigger | Typical Reward |
|---------|----------------|
| Completing a major objective | SP + AP |
| Finishing a story arc | SP + AP + SPARKLE |
| Surviving a harrowing situation | AP or SPARKLE |
| Significant downtime (weeks/months) | SP |
| Exceptional roleplay moment | AP (bonus) |
| GM's discretion | Any combination |

**No XP tracking** — the GM decides when advancement happens based on narrative pacing.

---

## What You Gain

### Skill Points (SP)

**SP** increases your level in Skills, unlocking more Abilities.

| Spend | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| 1 SP | +1 level in a Skill you already have |
| 2 SP | +1 level in a new Skill (first level costs extra) |

*Variant: First level costs 1 SP to encourage experimentation.*

As your Skill level increases:
- **Passive Abilities** unlock automatically
- **TRAINED Abilities** become available (but still cost AP)

### Ability Points (AP)

**AP** unlocks TRAINED abilities — powerful options that require investment.

| Spend | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| 1-3 AP | Unlock one TRAINED ability (cost varies by ability) |

AP is **scarce** — you won't unlock everything. This forces identity:
- "I'm the Gunslinger who took Fan the Hammer and Deadeye"
- "I'm the Gunslinger who took Trick Shot and went into Duelist"

### SPARKLE Points

**SPARKLE** increases your raw attributes.

| Spend | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| 1 SPARKLE | +1 to a generalized SPARKLE attribute |
| 1 SPARKLE | +1 to a specific SubSPARKLE |

**Strategic choice:**
- **Generalized** — always applies, broad benefit
- **SubSPARKLE** — higher ceiling for specific applications

*Example: Knowledge 4 (Generalized 2, Arcane 1, Systematics 1)*

---

## Advancement Pacing

| Stage | Sessions | SP | AP | SPARKLE | What It Feels Like |
|-------|----------|----|----|---------|-------------------|
| Fresh | 0 | — | — | — | Competent adventurer, defined concept |
| Seasoned | ~5 | +3 | +2 | +1 | Specialization emerging, signature moves |
| Veteran | ~15 | +6 | +4 | +2 | Multiple skill trees, flexible toolkit |
| Elite | ~30 | +10 | +6 | +4 | Master of your domains, feared/respected |
| Legendary | ~50+ | +15 | +8 | +6 | Campaign-defining power, world-shaking |

*These are guidelines — pacing depends on campaign.*

---

## Advancement at the Table

### Between Sessions
The GM announces rewards at session end:

> "That was a big one — you all get **1 SP and 1 AP**. We'll do advancement at the start of next session."

### During Downtime
If the fiction has significant time skips:

> "Three months pass while you recover. Everyone gains **2 SP** from training."

### Catch-Up Mechanics
If a new player joins or someone misses sessions:

- New characters start at the **party's average** minus a small amount
- Missed sessions might grant partial SP/AP
- Or just hand-wave it — Gryst isn't a numbers race

---

## Skill Progression Deep Dive

As you invest SP in a Skill, you unlock tiers of Abilities:

```
SKILL LEVEL → UNLOCKS
─────────────────────────────────────
1            Tier 1 abilities (basics)
2            Tier 1 abilities
3            Tier 2 abilities (intermediate)  
4            Tier 2 abilities
5            Tier 3 abilities (advanced)
6+           Tier 3 abilities, mastery perks
```

**Passive abilities** auto-unlock. **TRAINED abilities** require AP.

### Example: Arcanist Skill

| Level | Ability | Type | TRAINED? | AP |
|-------|---------|------|----------|-----|
| 1 | Arcane Sense | Perk | No | — |
| 1 | Arcane Bolt | Gambit | No | — |
| 2 | Gryst Attunement | Perk | No | — |
| 3 | Arcane Shield | Gambit | Yes | 1 |
| 3 | Elemental Affinity | Perk | Yes | 1 |
| 5 | Reality Tear | Gambit | Yes | 2 |
| 5 | Gryst Mastery | Perk | Yes | 2 |

---

## Power Curve Design

### Early Game (Sessions 1-5)
- Characters have 2-3 Skills at low levels
- Core Gambits from equipment + starting abilities
- Learning the Momentum system

### Mid Game (Sessions 6-20)
- Characters have 3-5 Skills, some at moderate levels
- Signature TRAINED abilities define playstyle
- SubSPARKLE specialization matters

### Late Game (Sessions 20+)
- Characters have broad or deep skill coverage
- Multiple high-AP abilities
- SPARKLE maxing specific areas
- GM introduces world-scale challenges

### No Hard Cap
There's no "max level" — but:
- Abilities have diminishing returns at high levels
- The story should escalate to match
- Retirement is always an option (legacy characters!)

---

## Respec & Mistakes

**No full respec** — your choices matter.

**But:**
- If a player genuinely isn't enjoying their build, work with the GM
- Narrative justification can unlock changes ("You trained with the monks...")
- Early campaign (first few sessions) allows more flexibility

# Character Creation

#ttrpg #rules #characters #sparkle #skills

## Overview

Character creation in Gryst takes **30-60 minutes** for new players, faster once you know the system. Characters start competent — you're not a farmhand with a stick, you're an adventurer with actual capabilities.

---

## The Steps

### 1. Concept

Who are you? What's your deal? This informs everything else.

*Examples: "A disgraced Consensus diplomat seeking redemption," "A Forgelord engineer who talks to their equipment," "A lucky gambler who stumbled into adventure."*

### 2. Species

The dominant race in Gryst are **Grystborn** — bipedal creatures evolved alongside Gryst energy. Humans are "hairless apekin" — not special, just one of many.

| Species | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Apekin | Humans and ape-descended folk |
| Foxkin | Clever, often traders or scouts |
| Wolfkin | Pack-oriented, loyalty-focused |
| Birdkin | Various avian types, often perceptive |
| *etc.* | *Content packs add more* |

Species may grant minor bonuses or narrative hooks, but no species is mechanically dominant.

### 3. SPARKLE Attributes

**SPARKLE** is your core attribute spread:

| Attribute | Abbr | Governs |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **S**trength | STR | Physical power, carrying capacity, melee impact |
| **P**erception | PER | Awareness, accuracy, noticing details |
| **A**gility | AGI | Speed, reflexes, **Momentum Gain** |
| **R**izz | RIZ | Social grace, charm, negotiation, performance |
| **K**nowledge | KNO | Education, memory, technical skill |
| **L**uck | LCK | Fate's favor, critical chances, "plot armor" |
| **E**ndurance | END | Health, stamina, resistance to harm |

#### SubSPARKLES

Each SPARKLE has **specializations** called SubSPARKLES. When you gain a SPARKLE point, you can:

1. **Keep it generalized** — adds to the base SPARKLE
2. **Specialize it** — assign to a SubSPARKLE

Your total in a SPARKLE = Generalized + Sum of all SubSPARKLES

**Example SubSPARKLES:**

| SPARKLE | SubSPARKLES |
|---------|-------------|
| Knowledge | Arcane, Systematics, History, Medicine |
| Perception | Visual, Auditory, Intuition |
| Rizz | Persuasion, Intimidation, Performance |
| Strength | Brute, Athletic |
| Agility | Reflexes, Stealth, Acrobatics |
| Endurance | Fortitude, Pain Tolerance |
| Luck | Fortune, Tempting Fate |

*Content packs can define additional SubSPARKLES.*

#### Starting SPARKLE

At character creation, you receive **X SPARKLE points** to distribute. The GM may use:

- **Standard Array:** Predetermined spread
- **Point Buy:** Spend from a pool with costs
- **Roll:** 4d6 drop lowest, assign as desired

---

### 4. Skills (The Multiclass System)

**Skills** are Gryst's class system — except there are no classes, only skills you invest in. Want to be a gun-slinging arcanist gambler? Put points in Gunslinger, Arcanist, and Gambler.

Each Skill is a **tree of Abilities** (Perks and Gambits) that unlock as your skill level increases.

#### Skill Points (SP)

You gain **SP** at character creation and when you advance. Spending SP in a skill increases your level in that skill.

#### Abilities

Every Skill contains **Abilities** — things you can do or passive benefits you have.

| Ability Type | Description |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Perk** | Passive benefit or feature |
| **Gambit** | An action you can take (adds to your Gambit library) |

Each Ability has:
- **Skill Requirement** — minimum skill level to access
- **SPARKLE Requirements** (optional) — may need certain attributes
- **TRAINED tag** (optional) — requires AP to unlock

#### Passive vs. Trained

- **Passive Abilities:** Automatically available when you meet the skill requirement
- **Trained Abilities:** Must spend **Ability Points (AP)** to unlock, even if you meet requirements

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GUNSLINGER SKILL                                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Level 1:                                        │
│   ✓ Quick Draw (Perk) — passive                 │
│   ✓ Shoot (Gambit) — passive                    │
│                                                 │
│ Level 2:                                        │
│   ✓ Steady Aim (Perk) — passive                 │
│   ⚡ Trick Shot (Gambit) — TRAINED, costs 1 AP  │
│                                                 │
│ Level 3:                                        │
│   ⚡ Fan the Hammer (Gambit) — TRAINED, 2 AP    │
│      Requires: AGI 3+                           │
│                                                 │
│ Level 5:                                        │
│   ⚡ Deadeye (Perk) — TRAINED, 2 AP             │
│      Requires: PER 4+, Visual subSPARKLE 2+    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Ability Points (AP)

**AP** is spent to unlock TRAINED abilities. You gain AP:
- At character creation
- Upon advancement
- As GM rewards for exceptional play

AP is precious — you can't unlock every TRAINED ability, so choose what defines your character.

---

### 5. Equipment (Loculi & Modules)

See [[Modules-and-Loculi]] for details.

Starting equipment is determined by:
- **Background/Concept** — a soldier starts with weapons, a scholar with books
- **Skill purchases** — some skills grant starting gear
- **GM discretion** — appropriate to the campaign's starting point

Remember: **Loculi are objects** (weapons, armor, tools) that **hold Modules** which **grant Gambits**.

---

### 6. Derived Stats

Some stats are calculated from your SPARKLE and other choices:

| Derived Stat | Formula |
|--------------|---------|
| **Momentum Gain** | Base + AGI modifier |
| **Health** | Base + END modifier |
| **Stamina** | Base + END + STR modifiers |

*Exact formulas TBD based on playtesting.*

### 7. Personality & Backstory

Answer these prompts (or similar):

1. **What do you want?** (A goal)
2. **What do you fear?** (A vulnerability)
3. **Who do you know?** (A connection to the world)
4. **What's your quirk?** (A memorable trait)

### 8. Connections

How do you know the other PCs? The GM may have you:

# Core Mechanic

#ttrpg #rules #mechanics #dice

Gryst uses a **two-axis resolution system**. One axis measures fate — whether your intent lands at all. The other measures physics — how reality responds when it does.

---

## The Two Dice

### Tessera Fati — The Dice of Fate

*"Did it happen?"*

The **Tessera Fati** resolve whether your action succeeds. They are **undefined by design** — the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) selects whatever dice fit the dramatic weight of the moment. A d4, d6, d10, d20, percentile, coin flip. Fate is negotiable.

These dice map to the **four outcomes**:

| Outcome | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Critical Success** | *Triumphus* | Exceptional result, bonus effects |
| **Success** | *Successus* | You achieve what you wanted |
| **Failure** | *Clades* | You don't achieve it, complications may arise |
| **Critical Failure** | *Calamitas* | Disaster, significant consequences |

The Tessera Fati are the **drama engine**. The universe deciding if your story goes the way you intended.

### Icosaedrum Magnitudinis — The d20 of Magnitude

*"How much?"*

The **Icosaedrum Magnitudinis** (also called the *Icosaedrum Grysti*) is always a d20. When you reach into reality and twist Gryst, this die measures how hard the universe pushes back. How far does your will extend? How much force? How much power?

The Icosaedrum is the **physics engine**. Reality is consistent. Gryst responds predictably to manipulation. Physics doesn't negotiate.

## Where the Structure Lives

The two dice systems are resolution *engines* — but the structure lives in **Gambits**.

A Gambit isn't just "roll to see what happens." It's a complete package:
- The **action** (what you're doing)
- The **four outcomes** (what happens on Triumphus, Successus, Clades, Calamitas)
- The **Mag formulas** (how the Icosaedrum calculates effects)

When a Module grants you a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit), you're getting pre-authored outcomes, not improvising them at the table. The Tessera Fati just selects which outcome fires. The Icosaedrum calculates the numbers within that outcome.

See the [Gambits](rules/GAMBITs.md) page for the full breakdown.

## Resolution Flow

A typical action involves both axes:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE FLOW                                           │
│                                                    │
│  TESSERA FATI: Did the attack hit?                 │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Triumphus / Successus / Clades / Calamitas        │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  If hit → ICOSAEDRUM MAGNITUDINIS determines       │
│           damage, range, effect intensity          │
│                                                    │
│  Fate resolves WHETHER.                            │
│  Magnitude resolves HOW MUCH.                      │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Fate is primary.** The Tessera Fati resolve whether something occurs. The Icosaedrum kicks in afterward to determine the magnitude of effects.

## Tessera Fati: The Drama Engine

### Any Dice, Any Distribution

As long as all parties agree on a mapping to the four outcomes, **any dice work**:

| Method | How It Works |
|--------|--------------|
| **d4** | 1 = Calamitas, 2 = Clades, 3 = Successus, 4 = Triumphus |
| **d6** | 1 = Calamitas, 2-3 = Clades, 4-5 = Successus, 6 = Triumphus |
| **d20** | 1 = Calamitas, 2-7 = Clades, 8-17 = Successus, 18-20 = Triumphus |
| **Percentile** | [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) defines ranges based on difficulty |
| **2d6** | Bell curve distribution, [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) defines thresholds |
| **Coin** | Heads = Successus, Tails = Clades (no crits) |
| **Custom** | Whatever the table agrees on |

The undefined nature of the Tessera Fati is intentional. Fate is mutable, negotiated, situation-dependent.

### The CHORAGVS Decides

The **[<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) determines the best dice and distribution** for any given Gambit. Factors:

- **Difficulty** — Harder tasks might have narrower success ranges
- **Risk** — High-stakes actions might have wider Calamitas ranges
- **Drama** — Sometimes you want extremes more likely
- **Speed** — d4 is faster than percentile calculation

### Negotiated Gambits

When a player proposes a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit), the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) and player **come to a consensus** on what's fair:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GAMBIT NEGOTIATION                                 │
│                                                    │
│  Player: "I want to leap across the chasm and      │
│           grab the artifact before the cultist."   │
│                                                    │
│  DM: "Okay, that's risky. Let's use a d6:          │
│       1 = Fall into chasm (Calamitas)              │
│       2-3 = Miss the grab, both scrambling         │
│       4-5 = You get the artifact                   │
│       6 = Get it AND knock cultist back"           │
│                                                    │
│  Player: "Deal."                                   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This negotiation IS the game. The Tessera Fati provide structure; the table provides judgment.

## Icosaedrum Magnitudinis: The Physics Engine

### Always d20

The Icosaedrum Magnitudinis is **always a d20**. Reality is consistent.

Players already bring d20s for tracking Momentum. We use them for all Mag-related calculations too.

### What Uses the Icosaedrum?

**Effect calculations** that scale with Magnitūdō:

```
Damage = Base + (Effective Mag × multiplier)
Range = Base + (Effective Mag × 5) meters
Duration = Effective Mag rounds
```

When you need to **roll for variable magnitude** (damage, contested effects):

- Damage roll: `d20 + Weapon Mag` vs armor/threshold
- Contested effect: Both sides roll `d20 + Mag`, higher wins
- Variable outcome: `d20 + Mag` determines intensity

The Icosaedrum handles **numbers**, not success/failure. That's the Tessera Fati's domain.

## Putting It Together

### Example: Rifle Attack (Module GAMBIT)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ SETUP                                                       │
│                                                             │
│   LOCULUS: Standard Rifle Frame (the object itself)         │
│   MODULE: Conventional Firearm Mechanism [Rifle]            │
│                                                             │
│   This Module grants: "Fire Conventional Single Round"      │
│   with pre-authored outcomes for rifles                     │

1. Player: "I shoot the cultist with my rifle."

2. CHORAGVS checks the GAMBIT: "Fire Conventional Single Round [Rifle]"
   → Outcomes are pre-defined by the Module
   → CHORAGVS selects Tessera Fati: "Roll d6."

3. Player rolls Tessera Fati: 5 (Successus)
   → The Gambit's Successus outcome: "Hit, deal damage based on Mag"

4. Player calculates damage using Icosaedrum:
   → Rifle Mag 4 + Marksmanship skill +2 = 6 damage

5. DM: "The cultist takes 6 damage and staggers back."
```

### Example: Triumphus with Module GAMBIT

```
1. Player rolls Tessera Fati: 6 on d6 (Triumphus!)

2. CHORAGVS checks the Module GAMBIT's Triumphus outcome:
   → "Fire Conventional Single Round [Rifle]" says:
     "Clean hit + penetration (ignores light cover/armor)"

3. Player calculates: 6 base damage, penetrates the cultist's vest

4. CHORAGVS expands the interface: "The round punches through their 
   armor — they stumble, clutching the wound. Blood on the wall."
```

### Example: Thermal Lance (Different Module, Different Gambit)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Different Module = Different Gambit = Different Outcomes    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

1. Player: "I fire my thermal lance at the cultist."

2. CHORAGVS checks the GAMBIT: "Focused Burn" from Thermal Lance Array
   → Different outcomes than a conventional firearm

3. Player rolls Tessera Fati: 6 (Triumphus!)
   → Gambit's Triumphus: "Target ignites, ongoing burn damage"

4. DM: "The beam catches them center mass — they ignite. 
   Fire spreads to their robes. They're screaming."
```

### Example: Contested Effect

```
1. Psion uses "Mind Probe" Gambit from their Psi-Amp Module.

2. DM: "Roll opposing Icosaedra — your will against their mental defenses."
   → Both roll d20 + their relevant Mag

3. Psion: d20 (14) + Psi-Amp Mag (3) = 17
   Knight: d20 (11) + Mental Resistance (2) = 13

4. Psion wins by 4 — per the GAMBIT, the probe holds for 4 rounds.
```

### Example: No Module (Core GAMBIT Fallback)

```

   → No unarmed combat Module equipped

3. Player rolls: 4 (Successus)

4. DM: "You connect. Base unarmed damage... that's 2. 
   He reels but he's not down."
```

## Design Philosophy

### Why Two Axes?

The separation reflects a deeper truth about how stories work:

**Fate** is capricious, negotiable, and dramatic. The same action might succeed gloriously or fail catastrophically depending on the moment. This is why the Tessera Fati are undefined — they flex to serve the narrative.

**Physics** is consistent, calculable, and fair. When you channel Gryst, the universe responds predictably. A fireball with Mag 5 always has roughly the same destructive potential. This is why the Icosaedrum is always d20 — reliability in chaos.

Together, they create tension: fate decides *if* you succeed, physics decides *what that means*.

### Why Dice-Agnostic Fate?

1. **DM Control** — The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) can tune probability to the fiction
2. **Flexibility** — Different situations deserve different odds
3. **Negotiation as Play** — Discussing the gambit IS roleplaying
4. **No Universal Balance** — Not everything should have equal odds

### Why d20 for Magnitude?

1. **Consistency** — One die type for all physics calculations
2. **Availability** — Already at the table for Momentum tracking
3. **Tradition** — Familiar to most players
4. **Range** — 1-20 + mods provides meaningful spread

### The Consensus

The system is named after a fictional galactic bureaucracy, and appropriately, it runs on **consensus**:

- Players and DM agree on Gambits
- Players and DM agree on which Tessera Fati to use
- Players and DM agree on outcome ranges
- Rules provide framework, not dictation

# Gambits

#ttrpg #rules #core #gambit

## What is a GAMBIT?

A **Gambit** is the atomic unit of resolution — any action with uncertain outcome that matters to the story.

But a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) is more than just "roll to see what happens." A well-designed Gambit contains:

- **The action itself** — what you're doing
- **All four outcomes** — pre-defined results for Triumphus, Successus, Clades, Calamitas
- **Magnitūdō formulas** — how the Icosaedrum calculates damage, range, duration, etc.

When a Module grants you a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit), you're getting a complete package. The Thermal Lance Mk.II doesn't just say "deal damage on a hit" — it says:

> **Triumphus:** Target ignites, taking ongoing burn damage  
> **Successus:** Clean hit, deal Mag × 2 thermal damage  
> **Clades:** Miss, beam scorches the environment  
> **Calamitas:** Weapon overheats, disabled for one round

The outcomes are authored into the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit). The Tessera Fati just picks which one fires.

But these outcomes are **interfaces, not laws**. They provide *intent* and *baseline results* — what usually happens when you use this Gambit. The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) can override them dynamically when something else would serve the story better.

"Target ignites" is the default. But maybe this time, your Triumphus sets off a chain reaction that blows the fuel depot. Or your Calamitas isn't "weapon overheats" — it's "the beam refracts and alerts every guard in the compound." The authored outcome is a starting point; the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) can pivot when inspiration strikes.

This is where emergent stories happen. The Gambit tells you *what usually occurs*. The table discovers *what actually happens*.

## Gambit Outcomes

Every Gambit has four possible outcomes:

| Outcome | Latin | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Critical Success** | *Triumphus* | Exceptional result, bonus effects |
| **Success** | *Successus* | You achieve what you wanted |
| **Failure** | *Clades* | You don't achieve it, complications may arise |
| **Critical Failure** | *Calamitas* | Disaster, significant consequences |

These aren't generic categories you improvise at the table — they're **pre-defined** for each [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit). A rifle's Attack Gambit has different outcomes than a social manipulation Gambit or a piloting maneuver Gambit.

## Where Gambits Come From

### Module GAMBITs (Primary Source)

The vast majority of Gambits come from **Modules** — your equipment, abilities, implants, spells, tech.

When you equip a Module, you gain access to its GAMBITs. Each Module defines exactly what actions it enables and what the four outcomes are:

| Module | Gambit | Triumphus | Calamitas |
|--------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Thermal Lance Mk.II | Burn Attack | Target ignites | Weapon overheats |
| Grav-Boots | Assisted Leap | Graceful arc, bonus distance | Ankle twist, prone |
| Psi-Amp | Mind Probe | Full memory access | Psychic feedback |
| Shield Drone | Intercept | Block + riposte opening | Drone disabled |

This is where **character builds diverge**. Your Thermal Lance does THIS on a Triumphus. My Cryo-Rifle does THAT. The Gambits are part of what you're buying when you spend Complexitās on a Module.

### Core GAMBITs (Utility Fallbacks)

Some Gambits are so universal they exist outside any specific Module:

- **Defense** — Dodge, block, or resist when you have no specific defensive Module
- **Skill Check** — Attempt a task based on raw ability
- **Social** — Persuade, deceive, or intimidate without specialized gear
- **Movement** — Navigate difficult terrain without movement-enhancing Modules

These are **fallbacks**. If you have a Module that grants a better version (Shield Drone's Intercept vs. generic Defense), use the Module [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit).

### Improvised Gambits (Anything Else)

Anyone at the table — player OR GM — can propose entirely new Gambits from nothing. Negotiate the outcomes, agree on the Tessera Fati, resolve on the spot.

> "I want to swing from the chandelier and kick the baron out the window."

That's not in any Module. But it's a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) now. Define the four outcomes together, roll, and see what happens.

## Three Layers of Flexibility

Even within this structure, the system flexes:

### 1. Pre-Authored (The Floor)

Module GAMBITs and Core GAMBITs ship with fully-defined outcomes. Pick up your rifle, you know exactly what its Attack Gambit does. This is your baseline — use it most of the time. It keeps play flowing.

### 2. GM-Modified (The Flex)

The GM can twist outcomes in the moment when it makes the session better.

Maybe your Thermal Lance's Calamitas isn't "weapon overheats" this time — it's "the beam refracts off that crystal wall and hits your ally" because that's more interesting right now.

**But respect the build.** (See GM Guidance below.)

### 3. Improvised (The Ceiling)

Propose new Gambits from nothing. Negotiate outcomes. Roll. This is for the moments no Module anticipated.

## Declaring an Improvised Gambit

When proposing a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) from scratch, define:

1. **Intent:** What are you trying to accomplish?
2. **Approach:** How are you doing it?
3. **Stakes:** What's at risk?

The table then negotiates:
- The **four outcomes** (Triumphus, Successus, Clades, Calamitas)
- Which **Tessera Fati** to use (d4? d6? d20? coin?)
- The **probability ranges** for each outcome

### Example: Improvised Gambit

> **"I want to leap across the chasm and grab the artifact before the cultist."**
> 
> - **Intent:** Get the artifact first
> - **Approach:** Athletic leap
> - **Stakes:** Fall into the chasm / cultist gets it
>
> **Negotiated outcomes:**
> - **Triumphus:** Grab it AND knock the cultist back  
> - **Successus:** Grab the artifact  
> - **Clades:** Miss the grab, both scrambling for it  
> - **Calamitas:** Fall into the chasm
>
> **DM:** "Roll d6. 1 is Calamitas, 2-3 is Clades, 4-5 is Successus, 6 is Triumphus."

## GM Guidance: Modification vs. Trust

The GM has full authority to modify Gambits and create new ones. But with great power comes great responsibility.

### When Modification is Good

- The pre-written outcome doesn't fit the fiction (you're in zero-G, "fall prone" doesn't make sense)
- A more interesting consequence suggests itself (the crystal wall refraction)
- The player suggests a creative twist and you agree it's better
- The situation has unique stakes that override the generic outcome

### When Modification Undermines Trust

- A player spent Complexitās on a Module specifically because its Triumphus was powerful — and you keep rewriting it
- You're nerfing outcomes because the player is "winning too much"
- The modification removes player agency rather than enhancing drama
- It feels arbitrary rather than fictionally grounded

### The Golden Rule

> **Modify outcomes to fit the fiction, but respect the intent of a player's build.**

If someone built their character around a specific Gambit, they chose that Gambit *for a reason*. The outcomes they were promised are part of their character concept. Bending them occasionally for drama is fine. Routinely undermining them is a breach of trust.

## Design Philosophy

### The Gambit Does the Heavy Lifting

The Gambit is the real atomic unit of the system. It's simultaneously:
- The **action**
- The **outcome table**
- The **scaling formula**

The two dice systems (Tessera Fati and Icosaedrum) are just resolution engines that the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) plugs into. The structure lives in the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) itself.

### Outcomes Are Baselines, Not Laws

Pre-authored outcomes are **what usually happens** — not what *must* happen.

**A good roll is always good. A bad roll is always bad.** This is sacred.

What can change is the *narrative framing*, not the *quality* of the outcome:

- A Triumphus might be a clean critical hit — or a "miss" that accidentally hit a stealthed, more important target behind them. Either way, **it's good for you.**
- A Calamitas might be your weapon jamming — or the shot going wide and shattering the only window between you and vacuum. Either way, **it's bad for you.**

The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) can never say "you rolled Triumphus but actually it's bad." That's a betrayal. The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) *can* say "your Triumphus looked like a miss, but fate had other plans" — as long as the result is still a win.

The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) is encouraged to add **flair** when it creates a better moment:

- "Weapon overheats" → Does the heat blister your hands? Does the glow give away your position?
- "Target ignites" → Does the fire spread? Does someone scream?
- "Critical hit" → Did you find a weak point, or did fate intervene in a stranger way?

The authored outcomes are the baseline expectation — what happens 90% of the time. The other 10% is where memorable stories live.

### Gryst Lives and Dies by GM Quality

This is a feature, not a bug.

The system explicitly trusts the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) to create great sessions. The rules are scaffolding, not shackles. A great DM will use this freedom to craft incredible moments. A mediocre DM might flounder without guardrails.

**The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm)'s goal is to create a good session and a great story.**

Flair is good. Surprise is good. Unexpected consequences are good. What's *not* good is constantly treading on player agency until they feel powerless. The difference:

| Good Flair | Bad Habit |
|------------|-----------|
| Occasional narrative twist that creates a story beat | Routinely rewriting outcomes to nerf player success |
| "Your miss actually hit something interesting" | "Your hit actually missed because I said so" |
| Consequences that open new possibilities | Consequences that just punish creativity |
| Players excited by the unexpected | Players frustrated they can't rely on anything |

**The golden ratio:** Follow the authored outcomes most of the time, so that when you diverge, it *means* something.

### ANYTHING IS POSSIBLE

Gryst is a wacky reality. Instead of saying "you can't do that," stack the odds appropriately and let the dice decide. Life is unpredictable.

Mundane actions that don't normally require rolls can suddenly become Gambits if the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) thinks it adds spice. Use sparingly so it doesn't become tedious.

Conversely, Gambits can appear as boons — unexpected moments of dramatic opportunity that wouldn't normally exist.

### Stakes Matter

If failure doesn't matter, don't roll. Gambits are for moments where the outcome is uncertain AND meaningful.

### Four Tiers, Not Binary

The four-tier system (not binary pass/fail) creates narrative texture. A Clades isn't just "you fail" — it's "you fail AND here's how the situation shifts." A Triumphus isn't just "you succeed" — it's "you succeed AND here's your moment of glory."

---

## Quick Reference

```
GAMBIT = Action + Outcomes + Mag Formulas

Sources:
  MODULE GAMBITS — Primary. Equipment defines specific GAMBITs.
  CORE GAMBITS   — Fallbacks. Universal actions without Modules.
  IMPROVISED     — Anything else. Negotiate at the table.

Flexibility:
  1. Pre-authored — use the written outcomes most of the time
  2. GM-modified  — twist when it improves the session
  3. Improvised   — anyone can propose, negotiate together

Resolution:
  Tessera Fati → picks which outcome fires
  Icosaedrum   → calculates the numbers within that outcome

Golden Rule:
  Modify outcomes to fit the fiction,
  but respect the intent of a player's build.
```

# Modules & Loculi

#ttrpg #rules #modules #equipment #customization

## The Module System

Everything in Gryst that *does something* is built from **Modules**. Weapons, armor, Walkers, Heavenstriders, even a character's trained abilities — they're all defined by what modules they carry.

---

## What is a Module?

A **Module** is a discrete package of capability. It can represent:

- A weapon attachment
- A trained technique
- An enchantment
- A cybernetic implant
- A spiritual bond
- A mechanical subsystem

Modules don't care about the "magic vs. tech" distinction — they just define what you can do.

### Module Components

Every module can provide one or more of:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Gambits** | Actions you can take (with dice pools, Add-On costs, etc.) |
| **Stats** | Numerical bonuses or modifications |
| **Perks** | Passive effects that are always active |
| **Triggers** | Perks that activate Gambits under certain conditions |

### Example Module

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MODULE: Thermal Lance Mk.II                        │
│ Mass: 3                                            │
│ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                    │
│ GAMBIT: Focused Beam                               │
│   Add-On Cost: 6                                   │
│   Range: Medium                                    │
│   Effect: Deal thermal damage to single target     │
│   Triumphus: Target ignites (ongoing damage)       │
│   Calamitas: Weapon overheats (1 tick cooldown)    │
│                                                    │
│ STAT: +1 Damage vs. armored targets                │
│                                                    │
│ PERK: Heat Sink                                    │
│   After using Focused Beam, gain +2 to resist      │
│   cold-based effects until your next pivot.        │
│                                                    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## The Three Values

Every Module and Loculus has three core values:

| Value | Latin | What It Means |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| **Mass** | — | How much capacity it occupies |
| **Complexitās** | "complexity" | How costly/rare/unbelievable it is |
| **Magnitūdō** | "magnitude" | How good/powerful it is |

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE ECONOMY OF GRYST                               │
│                                                    │
│  MASS ────────► What can hold it?                  │
│                 (Loculus capacity)                 │
│                                                    │
│  COMPLEXITĀS ─► What does it cost?                 │
│                 (Market value, character creation) │
│                                                    │
│  MAGNITŪDŌ ───► How well does it perform?          │
│                 (Scales effects, modifies costs)   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Mass: How Scale Works

Every module has a **Mass** value — a simple integer representing how much space/capacity it requires.

```
Mass 1  ─  Tiny (small implant, minor enchantment)
Mass 2  ─  Light (sidearm attachment, trained technique)
Mass 3  ─  Standard (primary weapon module, significant system)
Mass 5  ─  Heavy (vehicle weapon, major enchantment)
Mass 8  ─  Massive (Walker-scale armament)
Mass 12 ─  Colossal (Heavenstrider primary system)
```

Mass is **abstract** — it's not just physical weight. A "heavy" psychic module might be Mass 5 because of the mental load it requires, not because it weighs anything.

---

## Complexitās: The Cost System

**Complexitās** represents how complex, rare, or reality-bending something is. It's the universal "cost" metric for the game.

### What Determines Complexitās?

| Factor | Low Complexitās | High Complexitās |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Believability** | Conventional mechanisms | Reality-warping effects |
| **Rarity** | Common materials/knowledge | Rare components/secrets |
| **Power** | Modest effects | Game-changing abilities |
| **Precision** | Crude, simple | Intricate, refined |

### Examples

```
Complexitās 2  ─  Conventional firearm mechanism
Complexitās 4  ─  Enhanced targeting system  
Complexitās 6  ─  Plasma generation array
Complexitās 10 ─  Gravity manipulation field
Complexitās 15 ─  Spatial warping teleporter
Complexitās 20 ─  Reality-rewriting artifact
```

### Calculating Total Complexitās

For a complete object (a weapon, a Walker, etc.):

```
Total Complexitās = Loculus Complexitās + Σ(Module Complexitās × Magnitūdō modifier)
```

A gun (simple Loculus) with basic modules = affordable.
A Walker (complex Loculus) with advanced modules = expensive.

### What Complexitās Is Used For

| Context | How It's Used |
|---------|---------------|
| **Market Value** | Higher Complexitās = higher price |
| **Character Creation** | Budget of Complexitās points to spend |
| **Crafting** | Required skill/resources scale with Complexitās |
| **Balancing** | GM tool for encounter design |
| **Availability** | Higher Complexitās = harder to find |

---

## Magnitūdō: The Scaling Number

**Magnitūdō** is a number that flows through all calculations. It represents how *good* something is, and every module defines how that number affects its performance.

### The Core Idea

Magnitūdō isn't a tier lookup — it's a **variable in your formulas**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MAGNITŪDŌ AS A NUMBER                              │
│                                                    │
│  Module defines: "Damage = Base + (Mag × 2)"       │
│                                                    │
│  Rusty sword (Mag 1):   Damage = 2 + (1 × 2) = 4   │
│  Quality sword (Mag 3): Damage = 2 + (3 × 2) = 8   │
│  Legend sword (Mag 6):  Damage = 2 + (6 × 2) = 14  │
│                                                    │
│  Same module, same formula, different Mag.         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Typical Magnitūdō Ranges

```
Mag 0-1  ─  Broken, rusted, barely functional
Mag 2-3  ─  Standard, common equipment
Mag 4-5  ─  High quality, well-crafted
Mag 6-8  ─  Exceptional, masterwork
Mag 9+   ─  Legendary, artifact-tier
```

But there's no hard cap — Magnitūdō can scale as high as the fiction allows.

### How Modules Use Magnitūdō

Each module defines its own formulas. Common patterns:

| Usage | Example Formula |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Direct bonus** | Accuracy = Mag |
| **Multiplied** | Damage = Mag × 2 |
| **Added to base** | Range = 10 + (Mag × 5) meters |
| **Reduced cost** | Add-On Cost = 8 - Mag (min 2) |
| **Dice scaling** | Roll = Mag d6 |

### Example Module

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MODULE: Precision Barrel                           │
│ Mass: 2 | Base Complexitās: 3                      │
│ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                    │
│ STAT: Accuracy Bonus = Mag                         │
│ STAT: Effective Range = 20 + (Mag × 10) meters     │
│                                                    │
│ GAMBIT: Aimed Shot                                 │
│   Add-On Cost: 7 - floor(Mag / 2)                  │
│   Damage: 1d6 + Mag                                │
│                                                    │
│ PERK (Optional, Mag 5+): True Strike               │
│   Once per Resolution, reroll an attack gambit     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The optional tier-locked perk is still possible, but the primary scaling is through the number itself.

### Character Stats Modify Magnitūdō

Here's the key: in combat, your character's relevant stats can **modify the effective Magnitūdō** of your equipment.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ EFFECTIVE MAGNITŪDŌ IN COMBAT                      │
│                                                    │
│  Effective Mag = Base Mag + Stat Modifier          │
│                                                    │
│  Example:                                          │
│    Sword Base Mag: 3                               │
│    Character's Might: +2                           │
│    Effective Mag: 5                                │
│                                                    │
│  The same sword hits harder in skilled hands.      │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This means:
- A novice with a masterwork weapon uses its base Mag
- A master with a rusty sword can still be deadly (stat modifier compensates)
- A master with a masterwork weapon is terrifying (stacking Mag)

### Tags: What Modifies Mag

Every Gambit has **Tags** that determine which skills can modify Magnitūdō.

**See [Tags](rules/Tags.md) for the full tag system.**

Quick example:
```
Gambit: "Aimed Shot" | Tags: [Marksmanship]
Your Marksmanship skill: +3 | Weapon Mag: 4
Effective Mag: 7
```

### Complexitās Modifier

Magnitūdō also affects cost. Higher quality = more expensive:

```
Effective Complexitās = Base Complexitās × (1 + Mag/4)

Mag 0:  ×1.0  (baseline)
Mag 2:  ×1.5  
Mag 4:  ×2.0
Mag 6:  ×2.5
Mag 8:  ×3.0
```

### Example: The Same Gun, Different Mag

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ RUSTY REVOLVER              │ MASTERWORK REVOLVER           │
│ Magnitūdō: 1                │ Magnitūdō: 6                  │
│ ────────────────────────────│──────────────────────────────│
│                             │                               │
│ Installed: Chamber Module   │ Installed: Chamber Module     │
│   Damage = 1d6 + Mag        │   Damage = 1d6 + Mag          │
│   → 1d6 + 1                 │   → 1d6 + 6                   │
│                             │                               │
│   Range = 10 + (Mag × 5)    │   Range = 10 + (Mag × 5)      │
│   → 15 meters               │   → 40 meters                 │
│                             │                               │
│   Add-On = 6 - floor(Mag/2) │   Add-On = 6 - floor(Mag/2)   │
│   → 6 (no reduction)        │   → 3 (fast to use)           │
│ ────────────────────────────│──────────────────────────────│
│ In the hands of a Gunslinger (Perception +3):              │
│   Effective Mag: 4          │   Effective Mag: 9            │
│   Damage: 1d6 + 4           │   Damage: 1d6 + 9             │
│   Range: 30 meters          │   Range: 55 meters            │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Loculus Magnitūdō

Loculi also have Magnitūdō, which typically affects:

- **Capacity** — Capacity = Base + Mag (higher quality = more slots)
- **Durability** — Damage resistance scales with Mag
- **Built-in Bonuses** — Some Loculi add their Mag to all installed modules

## Loculus: What Holds Modules

A **Loculus** (plural: **Loculi**) is anything that can hold modules. Think of it as a container with a capacity limit.

| Loculus Type | Typical Capacity | Examples |
|--------------|------------------|----------|
| **Personal** | 5-10 Mass | Character's equipped gear, innate abilities |
| **Weapon** | 3-8 Mass | A rifle, sword, or focus with attachment slots |
| **Armor** | 4-10 Mass | Protective gear with enhancement slots |
| **Walker** | 20-40 Mass | Piloted mecha with multiple hardpoints |
| **Heavenstrider** | 30-60 Mass | Flight-capable units with extensive systems |
| **Vehicle** | 15-30 Mass | Ground or sea vehicles |
| **Installation** | 50+ Mass | Stationary defenses, bases, ships |

### Loculus Rules

1. **Capacity** — A Loculus can hold modules up to its total capacity
2. **No Rigid Slots** — Modules exist arbitrarily within a Loculus; the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) determines what makes narrative sense
3. **Module Tags** — Modules have tags that define what they are (see below)
4. **Overflow** — You cannot exceed capacity; choose what to install

---

## Module Tags & Narrative Constraints

Modules have **tags** that define what type of component they are. These tags create soft constraints that the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) interprets narratively.

**See [Tags](rules/Tags.md) for the full tag system.**

### Example: The Cor System

Every Walker needs a **Cor** (heart/core) — a power source. All core modules are tagged `[Cor]`.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MODULE: Fusion Cor Mk.III                          │
│ Tags: [Cor] [Power] [Thermal]                      │
│ Mass: 8 | Complexitās: 15                          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Soft Constraints via Tags

The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) understands baseline expectations:
- "A Walker has 1 Cor" — this is the default
- "A Walker has 2 Arms" — this is the default

But modules can **modify these constraints** (e.g., Redundancy Protocol Cor Chambering allows an extra Cor — with trade-offs).

### Why No Rigid Slots?

Rigid slot systems limit creativity. Instead:
- **Tags define what something IS**
- **DM interprets what makes sense**
- **Modules can bend rules**
- **Flexibility enables weird builds**

---

## Required Disambiguation Canonization (RDC)

Details can remain undefined — until they matter. When you answer, it becomes permanent canon.

**See [Required Disambiguation Canonization](rules/Required-Disambiguation-Canonization.md) for the full RDC system.**

Quick summary: If you never specified where your Thermal Lance is mounted, you get to decide when the GM asks — but then it's locked forever. This creates "artificial luck" where undefined details can save you... but also lock you into consequences later.

Here's the elegant part: the same *conceptual* module can exist at different scales.

### Example: Plasma Repeater

│ For: Vehicle weapon Loculus                        │
│ Gambit: Rapid Fire (Add-On: 5)                     │
│ Effect: Multiple medium-damage hits                │
│ Perk: Suppression — targets take -1 to Add-On      │

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

The core identity ("plasma repeater that fires rapidly") stays consistent. The Mass, power, and additional perks scale with the Loculus it's designed for.

## Triggered Perks: Passive-to-Active

Some Perks can **trigger Gambits** under specific conditions. These are the bridge between passive effects and active abilities.

### Example: Reactive Shielding

```
PERK: Reactive Shielding
  When you take damage from a ranged attack, 
  you may immediately trigger the EMERGENCY BARRIER
  Gambit at no Add-On cost. (Once per Resolution phase)
```

This lets a passive defense become an active response without requiring the player to have held momentum for an Add-On.

## Design Notes

### Why "Loculus"?

Latin for "compartment" or "small box." It evokes both ancient mysticism and mechanical precision — fitting for a world where magic and tech are the same.

### Why Mass Instead of Slots?

Slots are rigid ("you have 3 weapon slots"). Mass is flexible ("you have 8 capacity; fill it however you want"). This allows for:

- **Trade-offs** — One big module vs. many small ones
- **Scaling** — Same math works from characters to Walkers
- **Creativity** — Players aren't locked into predetermined builds

Common modules are widely available. Rare modules require quests, crafting, or significant resources. Legendary modules are campaign-defining.

# MOMENTVM & Turn System

#ttrpg #rules #core #momentum #initiative

## Overview

Turns are not static initiative order — they're earned through **[<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) (MTM)**, a racing resource that determines when PERSONAe act. Combat flows through two phases: **[<span style="color:#ff3333">PHASEACCRETIALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#phaseaccretialis-accrual-phase) (Accrual Phase)** where everyone races to 20, and **Resolution** where the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) (Queue) builds and resolves.

---

## MOMENTVM Basics

### MOMENTVM Gain
Each [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) (Character) has a **[<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) Gain** stat:

```
MOMENTVM Gain = Agility + [Modifiers]
```

This represents how quickly a [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) builds toward their next action.

### Tracking MOMENTVM
- Use a **d20 as a tracker** (not rolled — just a counter)
- Start at 0 (or determined by setup)
- Track carries over between phases
- [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) can go **negative** (see [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) Tax)

---

## COHORS & CONCORDIA

Combat is organized into **COHORTes (Cohorts)** — groups of PERSONAe working toward shared goals.

### What is a COHORS?

A [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) is any group with **[<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation) (Cooperation)** — the ability to freely coordinate during the [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) window.

**Typical setup:**
- 1 Player [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) vs 1 [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) (DM) Enemy [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)
- 2 Player COHORTes vs 1 [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) Enemy [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) (players split on goals)
- 1 Player [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) vs 2 [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) Enemy COHORTes (multiple factions)

### Forming & Splitting COHORTes

COHORTes can form or split **dynamically** during play:

- **Split:** Players disagree on a goal mid-combat → they can split into separate COHORTes
- **Merge:** Former enemies align against a common threat → new [<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation)
- **Betrayal:** A [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) switches sides → joins a different [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)'s [<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation)

The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) adjudicates when [<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation) makes narrative sense.

### What CONCORDIA Gives You

Within your [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) (PERSONAe you have [<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation) with):

1. **Free ADDENDa** — No special requirements to add onto a cohortmate's [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue)
2. **Flexible Ordering** — Cohortmates can negotiate who slots where, regardless of raw [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) order
3. **Shared Strategy** — You're building the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) together

Without [<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation) (different [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)):

1. **Locked Out** — You cannot [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) to their [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue)...
2. **Unless You Create an [<span style="color:#9d64f2">OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#occ-si-tvrbida-chaotic-moment)** — [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) GAMBITs open the door (see below)

## The MOMENTVM Race

### PHASEACCRETIALIS (Accrual Phase)

All PERSONAe add their [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) Gain **simultaneously**. Keep adding until someone reaches 20+.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASEACCRETIALIS (Accrual Phase)                   │
│                                                    │
│  Everyone ACCRVEs MOMENTVM simultaneously          │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Someone hits 20+ → They become PERSONA CARDINALIS │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  → Proceed to Resolution Phase                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### PERSONA CARDINALIS (Pivotal Character)

The first [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) to reach 20+ becomes the **[<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) (Pivotal Character)** — they've won initiative and will lead this resolution phase.

**Important:** PERSONAe who didn't reach 20 **keep their full [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm)**. They didn't spend anything to lose the race — their [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) is still theirs to spend on ADDENDa.

### Multiple PERSONAe at 20+

If multiple PERSONAe cross 20 on the same "tick":

1. **First tiebreaker:** Higher remainder after subtracting 20
2. **Second tiebreaker:** Roll a d6, higher wins

The winner becomes [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal); the others queue behind them.

### Example

| [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) | [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) Gain | Before | After ACCRVEing | Remainder | Result |
|---------|---------------|--------|-----------------|-----------|--------|
| Raven | 6 | 15 | 21 | **1** | Queued 2nd |
| Wolf | 4 | 12 | 16 | — | Keeps 16, can [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) |
| Hawk | 5 | 17 | 22 | **2** | **[<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal)** |

Hawk becomes [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) (remainder 2). Raven queues behind (remainder 1). Wolf didn't hit 20, keeps their 16 [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm).

## Resolution Phase: The TABVLA

When someone becomes [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal), combat shifts from racing to **[<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) building**.

### Building the TABVLA (Queue)

1. **[<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) declares their [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit)** (e.g., "I shoot my blaster")
2. **[<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) window opens**, ordered by highest [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) → lowest
3. Each [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) may spend [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) to [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon)
4. ADDENDa slot **BEFORE** or **AFTER** the [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal)'s [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) in the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue)
   - Unless the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) specifies its ordering when added on
5. The [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) is now a **queue of GAMBITs** waiting to resolve

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TABVLA BUILDING                                    │
│                                                    │
│  CARDINALIS: "I shoot my blaster" [SLOT 1]         │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  ADDENDVM window (highest MOMENTVM → lowest):      │
│    Ally A (14 MTM): "I aim assist" [BEFORE]        │
│    Ally B (10 MTM): "I cover fire" [AFTER]         │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Final TABVLA:                                     │
│    1. Aim Assist (Ally A)                          │
│    2. Shoot Blaster (CARDINALIS)                   │
│    3. Cover Fire (Ally B)                          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### TABVLA Resolution

The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) resolves the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue), narrating it as **one fluid sequence**. This isn't "you go, then you go" — it's a single cinematic moment built collaboratively.

**Fizzle Rule:** If a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) no longer applies when it resolves (target dead, situation changed, etc.), it **fizzles** and returns **half the [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) spent, rounded up**. The [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) lost some [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) because they were planning on doing something that no longer was possible — they're caught flatfooted.

### Free Fizzle: Conditional ADDENDa

When adding a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) to the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue), you can declare it **conditional** on a specific factor. If that condition isn't satisfied, you **fizzle for free** — full [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) refund.

**Requirements for Free Fizzle:**
- The condition must be **clearly defined** when adding to the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue)
- The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) judges whether the condition was satisfied
- The player doesn't get to retroactively decide "actually, that counts"

**Why this exists:** It allows players to plan reactive GAMBITs without wasting resources if the situation doesn't develop as expected.

### After Resolution

1. Subtract 20 from the [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal)'s tracker
2. If another [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) is at 20+ → they become [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal), repeat Resolution
3. If no one is at 20+ → return to [<span style="color:#ff3333">PHASEACCRETIALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#phaseaccretialis-accrual-phase)

## ADDENDVM System

### How ADDENDVM Works

During the [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) window, PERSONAe may spend [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) to join the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue).

1. **Order:** Highest current [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) → lowest gets first choice
2. **Placement:** Choose to go BEFORE or AFTER in the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) (unless [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) specifies)
3. **Cost:** Subtract [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost from your [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) tracker
4. Your [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) enters the queue

### ADDENDVM Costs

Each [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) has an **[<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost** listed. Higher-impact GAMBITs cost more.

| [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) Type | Typical [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Minor [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) | 3-5 [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) |
| Standard [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) | 6-10 [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) |
| Major [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) | 11-15 [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) |

### ADDENDVM Strategy

- Spending [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) **delays your next turn** (you're further from 20)
- Coordinating ADDENDa creates powerful combo moments
- The fizzle refund protects you if the situation shifts
- Consider: spend now for teamwork, or save to become [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) yourself?

## [INTERVENTVS] & OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA

Some GAMBITs have the **[<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) (Intervention)** tag. [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) is how COHORTes break into each other's TABVLAe.

During the [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) window, you can only add onto PERSONAe you have **[<span style="color:#66023c">CONCORDIA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#concordia-cooperation)** with. If the [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) is in a different [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort), you're locked out.

### OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA (Chaotic Moment)

When a [<span style="color:#e2725b">PERSONA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#persona-character) uses an **[<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit)**, they force their way into the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue). [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) GAMBITs are responses to other GAMBITs. They create "moments of chaos" when careful plans break down. Putting the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) into this **[<span style="color:#9d64f2">OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#occ-si-tvrbida-chaotic-moment)** state allows non-[<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) COHORTes to join the fray!

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [INTERVENTVS] AS DOOR OPENER                       │
│                                                    │
│  COHORS 1 is CARDINALIS. COHORS 2 is locked out.   │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  COHORS 2 member uses [INTERVENTVS] GAMBIT!        │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA — THE DOOR IS NOW OPEN             │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Other COHORS 2 members can now ADDENDVM           │
│  (but they pay the [INTERVENTVS] Tax)              │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### [INTERVENTVS] Rules

1. **Requires [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) tag** — Only certain GAMBITs can open the door
2. **Pay normal [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost** — The intervener pays their [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit)'s cost
3. **Coupling** — Your [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) **couples** with the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) it's intervening (see below)
4. **Door opens for your [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)** — After your [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention), cohortmates can [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon)
5. **Post-Couple Ordering** — Intervening [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)'s follow-up GAMBITs must come AFTER the couple

### Coupling: The Heart of [INTERVENTVS]

When you use [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention), your [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) doesn't just slot into the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) — it **couples** with the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) you're intervening. The two resolve as a single dramatic unit.

**Why coupling matters:**
- The [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) IS the reaction — it doesn't make sense before the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) it's reacting to
- The chaos of the couple is what opens the door for your [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort)
- It creates a clear narrative beat: action → reaction → fallout

### The [INTERVENTVS] Tax

Once the door is open, your cohortmates can [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) even **without** [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) GAMBITs — but they pay an **[<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) Tax** on top of their normal [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost.

| Cost | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Intervener** | Normal [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost (they opened the door) |
| **Cohortmates** | Normal [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon) Cost **+ [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) Tax (5)** |

The [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) Tax is paid **after** the normal cost. This means [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) can go **negative**.

### Negative MOMENTVM

If the [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) Tax drops you below 0, track it as negative:
- `3 → 2 → 1 → 0 → -1 → -2 → -3...`
- Negative [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) means you're **far** from your next pivot — you'll be racing back up

### [INTERVENTVS] Failure Cascade

If an **[<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) itself fails** ([<span style="color:#8c0303">CLADES</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#clades-failure)/[<span style="color:#ff0000">CALAMITAS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#calamitas-calamity)), the [<span style="color:#9d64f2">OCCĀSIŌTVRBIDA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#occ-si-tvrbida-chaotic-moment) never materializes:
- The door never truly opened — there was no chaos to capitalize on
- **All ADDENDa from the intervening [<span style="color:#66023c">COHORS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cohors-cohort) free fizzle**

The intervener themselves **does not free fizzle** — they committed to the [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) regardless. Only the follow-up ADDENDa are affected.

### Example [INTERVENTVS] GAMBITs

- **Parry** ([<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention)): React to an incoming attack, reduce damage
- **Counterspell** ([<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention)): Interrupt enemy spellcasting
- **Intercept** ([<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention)): Move to block an attack targeting an ally
- **Force Reflector** ([<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention)): Redirect an energy attack
- **Overwatch** ([<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention)): Prepared shot triggers on enemy [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit)

## Why This System?

### Player Engagement
No one is "waiting for their turn." During [<span style="color:#ff3333">PHASEACCRETIALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#phaseaccretialis-accrual-phase), everyone's watching the race. During Resolution, everyone's deciding whether to [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon), where to slot in, how to build the [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue). The [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) weaves it all into one narrative beat.

### Dynamic Turn Order
Initiative isn't rolled once and forgotten. It shifts every phase based on [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) choices. Spend to help allies, and you delay yourself. Save up, and you'll be [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal) more often.

### Narrative Combat
The [<span style="color:#708090">TABVLA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#tabvla-queue) resolves as a single moment, not ping-pong turns. "Raven aims, Hawk fires, Wolf dives for cover" happens together — it *feels* like a [<span style="color:#68001a">SCAENA</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#scaena-scene) (Scene).

### Meaningful Choices
- Spend [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) to [<span style="color:#43b3ae">ADDENDVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#addendvm-addon)? Or save for your own turn?
- Slot before (set up the [<span style="color:#ffd500">CARDINALIS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#cardinalis-pivotal)) or after (capitalize on their [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit))?
- [<span style="color:#9d64f2">[INTERVENTVS]</span>](/codex/Cor/Tags.md#interventvs-intervention) an enemy? Worth the [<span style="color:#ff3333">MOMENTVM</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#momentvm-mtm) cost?

# Required Disambiguation Canonization (RDC)

#ttrpg #rules #rdc #narrative #core

Details can remain undefined — until they matter. When you're asked to specify something and you answer, that answer becomes permanent canon. This is the **Thread of Fate**.

---

## The Core Principle

> **When the DM asks for a detail and you answer, that answer becomes canon.**

Things that were undefined become defined. Things that are defined cannot be changed.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE THREAD OF FATE                                 │
│                                                    │
│  UNDEFINED ──────► DM ASKS ──────► YOU ANSWER      │
│                                          │         │
│                                          ▼         │
│                                    NOW CANON       │
│                                    (permanent)     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Example: Module Placement

```
SESSION 1:
  Player: "I fire my Thermal Lance."
  GM: "It hits. Moving on..."
  
  (Location of Thermal Lance: UNDEFINED)

SESSION 3:
  GM: "The enemy targets your right arm. What's mounted there?"
  Player: "My Thermal Lance."
  GM: "Okay, it's damaged."
  
  (Location of Thermal Lance: NOW CANON — right arm)
  
SESSION 4:
  Player: "Wait, I want my Thermal Lance on my left arm now."
  GM: "No — you declared it was on your right arm. That's canon."
```

---

## Why RDC?

### 1. Reduces Upfront Bookkeeping

You don't need to specify every detail at character creation. Fill in the blanks as they become relevant.

### 2. Emergent Detail

The world and your character fill in as you play. Details emerge from gameplay rather than being front-loaded.

### 3. Player Agency

You get to choose in the moment — when it matters most. It's your call.

### 4. Prevents Retroactive Convenience

Once you've declared something, you can't un-declare it. No "actually it was always on my other arm."

### 5. Artificial Luck

Undefined details let players "get lucky" in narratively interesting ways — but only once.

---

## The Lucky Shot: RDC as Narrative Fortune

Here's the fun part: RDC lets players make advantageous choices *in the moment* — and that's a feature, not a bug.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE LUCKY SHOT                                     │
│ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                    │
│ GM: "A shot rings out! Critical hit to your        │
│      Walker's RIGHT SHOULDER mount!"               │
│                                                    │
│ GM: "Wait... where are your Shoulder Missiles?     │
│      The loaded ones that would explode if hit?"   │
│                                                    │
│ Player: (hasn't defined this yet)                  │
│ Player: "Oh, those are on my LEFT shoulder."       │
│                                                    │
│ GM: (smiles) "Well, it's your lucky day. Your      │
│      unloaded decoy launcher got damaged — needs   │
│      repairs, but you avoided catastrophe!"        │
│                                                    │
│ (LEFT SHOULDER = Missiles is now CANON)            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Why This Works

The player didn't cheat — they made a choice about something that was genuinely undefined. But now:

- **The missiles are locked to LEFT shoulder forever**
- **Next time the left shoulder gets hit?** Boom.
- **The player used their "luck" — it's spent**

This creates **dramatic tension**: every undefined detail is a potential save... but also a potential lock-in that might hurt later.

---

## The Trade-Off

Players who **over-specify early** lose this flexibility. Everything is already canon — no room to maneuver.

Players who **stay vague** keep their options open — but risk the GM asking at the *worst possible moment*.

> **GM:** "The explosion tears through the cockpit. Where exactly is your ejection system?"
> 
> **Player:** "...I never said."
> 
> **GM:** "Then you get to decide right now. Choose wisely."

It's **Schrödinger's Loadout**: undefined until observed, then permanent.

---

## When RDC Applies

RDC can be invoked for any detail that was never specified:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Physical placement** | "Where is your module mounted?" |
| **Appearance** | "What does your character look like?" |
| **Backstory** | "Where did you get that weapon?" |
| **Relationships** | "How do you know this NPC?" |
| **Equipment details** | "What color is your Walker?" |
| **Location specifics** | "Which pocket is the key in?" |

If you never said, and it matters now, you get to decide — once.

---

## Tracking Canonized Details

PDA and character sheets need a **Notes** section for tracking RDC details:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ CANONIZED DETAILS                                  │
│ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                                    │
│ • Thermal Lance mounted on RIGHT ARM (Session 3)   │
│ • Cor positioned in upper torso (Session 5)        │
│ • Ejection hatch is on the BACK (Session 7)        │
│ • Character has a scar on left cheek (Session 2)   │
│ • Walker paint scheme: matte black (Session 4)     │
│ • Mentor's name: Instructor Vex (Session 8)        │
│                                                    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Include the session number so everyone remembers when details became canon.

---

## GM Guidance

### When to Invoke RDC

- **When a detail suddenly matters** — "Where's that component?"
- **When specificity creates drama** — "Who gave you that weapon?"
- **When players are being too vague** — "I need you to commit to something."

### When NOT to Invoke RDC

- **Trivial details** — Don't canonize everything; let flavor stay flexible
- **Mid-combat gotchas** — Don't weaponize RDC against players unfairly
- **Already established fiction** — If it was clearly implied, don't demand re-specification

### The Spirit of RDC

RDC exists to create interesting moments, not to trap players. If a player forgot they said something three sessions ago, be generous. If a player is clearly trying to game the system, push back.

The goal is collaborative storytelling, not legal proceedings.

---

## Design Notes

### Why "Required Disambiguation Canonization"?

Because it sounds appropriately formal and slightly arcane — fitting for a game called Gryst.

- **Required** — The DM is asking; you must answer
- **Disambiguation** — Clarifying something that was vague
- **Canonization** — Making it permanent, official, true

### Inspiration

This concept draws from:
- **Schrödinger's thought experiment** — Undefined until observed
- **Improv "yes, and"** — Building on what's established
- **Blades in the Dark's flashbacks** — Retroactive establishment (but RDC is present-tense)
- **OSR rulings over rules** — Embrace undefined space

RDC gives players agency while creating natural dramatic pressure. Use it to make your game more interesting.

# Skills

#ttrpg #rules #skills #abilities #multiclass

## Overview

**Skills** are Gryst's class system — except there are no classes, only skills you invest in.

Every Skill is a **tree of Abilities** (Perks and Gambits) that you unlock by:
1. Increasing your Skill level with **SP (Skill Points)**
2. Spending **AP (Ability Points)** on TRAINED abilities

You can invest in as many Skills as you want. There's no "multiclass penalty" — a Gunslinger-Arcanist-Gambler is a valid build.

---

## Skill Points (SP)

**SP** is the currency for Skill progression.

| Action | Cost |
|--------|------|
| Increase a Skill you have by 1 level | 1 SP |
| Learn a new Skill (first level) | 2 SP |

*Variant: First level costs 1 SP to encourage experimentation.*

**Gaining SP:**
- Character creation
- Milestone rewards from the GM
- Downtime training

---

## Skill Levels & Ability Tiers

As your Skill level increases, you unlock access to higher-tier Abilities:

| Skill Level | Tier | Ability Power |
|-------------|------|---------------|
| 1-2 | Tier 1 | Basic — core actions and simple perks |
| 3-4 | Tier 2 | Intermediate — signature moves |
| 5-6 | Tier 3 | Advanced — powerful, defining abilities |
| 7+ | Mastery | Capstones and legendary options |

---

## Abilities

**Abilities** are the actual things you get from Skills. There are two types:

### Perks (Passive)

Perks are always-on benefits or features:
- "You can see in dim light"
- "+1 to Initiative"
- "You never fail to notice traps"

### Gambits (Active)

Gambits are **actions** — they go into your Gambit library and can be used in play:
- "Fan the Hammer" (attack)
- "Arcane Shield" (defense)
- "Lucky Break" (utility)

*See [[Gambits]] for how Gambits work mechanically.*

---

## Passive vs. TRAINED

### Passive Abilities

**Automatically available** when you meet the Skill level requirement.

> "Gunslinger 1 grants Quick Draw (Perk) — you have it as soon as you have Gunslinger 1."

### TRAINED Abilities

**Require spending AP** to unlock, even if you meet the requirements.

> "Fan the Hammer is TRAINED and costs 2 AP. Even at Gunslinger 3, you don't have it until you spend the AP."

**Why TRAINED?**
- Forces meaningful choices
- Differentiates characters with the same Skills
- Represents dedicated practice beyond basic competence

---

## Ability Points (AP)

**AP** unlocks TRAINED abilities.

| Action | Cost |
|--------|------|
| Unlock a TRAINED ability | 1-3 AP (varies by ability) |

**Gaining AP:**
- Character creation
- Milestone rewards from the GM
- Exceptional roleplay (GM bonus)
- Special in-game training or events

**AP is scarce** — you won't unlock every TRAINED ability in every Skill. This forces identity.

---

## SPARKLE Requirements

Some Abilities have **SPARKLE requirements** in addition to Skill level:

```
Deadeye (Perk) — TRAINED, 2 AP
Requires: Gunslinger 5, PER 4+, Visual (SubSPARKLE) 2+
```

You must meet ALL requirements to unlock the ability:
- Gunslinger at level 5+
- Total Perception at 4+
- At least 2 points specifically in Visual SubSPARKLE

---

These are the default Skills. Content packs can add more.

### Exploration Skills
| Skill | Focus |
|-------|-------|
| **Scout** | Tracking, navigation, survival |

---

## Building Your Character

### Focused Build
Pick 1-2 Skills and go deep:
- High Skill levels = Tier 3 abilities
- Lots of TRAINED abilities in your specialty
- Risk: narrow, but powerful

### Broad Build  
Pick 3-5 Skills at low levels:
- Lots of Tier 1 abilities
- Flexibility for many situations
- Risk: jack of all trades, master of none

### Hybrid Build
1 deep Skill, 2-3 shallow Skills:
- Signature specialty + utility options
- Most common and recommended

---

## Skills & Equipment

Skills and [[Modules-and-Loculi|Loculi]] work together:

- **Skills grant Gambits** — inherent abilities you always have
- **Equipment grants Gambits** — abilities tied to your gear

A Gunslinger without a gun still has Quick Draw (the skill to draw fast) but needs a weapon to actually shoot.

Some Skill abilities **modify equipment Gambits**:
> "Deadeye: +2 Magnitūdō on ranged attacks from any weapon"

---

## Custom Skills

Content packs can define new Skills with their own Ability trees. The structure is always:

1. Skill name and theme
2. Tier 1-3 Abilities (Perks and Gambits)
3. Passive vs. TRAINED designation
4. SPARKLE requirements (if any)
5. AP costs for TRAINED abilities

*See GrystForge for the authoring tool.*

# SPARKLE Attributes

#ttrpg #rules #sparkle #attributes

## Overview

**SPARKLE** is the acronym for Gryst's seven core attributes:

| Letter | Attribute | Description |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **S** | Strength | Physical power, lifting, melee impact, carrying capacity |
| **P** | Perception | Awareness, accuracy, noticing details, reading situations |
| **A** | Agility | Speed, reflexes, coordination, **determines Momentum Gain** |
| **R** | Rizz | Social grace, charm, negotiation, performance, intimidation |
| **K** | Knowledge | Education, memory, technical skill, problem-solving |
| **L** | Luck | Fate's favor, critical chances, "things just work out" |
| **E** | Endurance | Health, stamina, resistance to harm, staying power |

---

## SubSPARKLES

Each SPARKLE can be specialized into **SubSPARKLES** — narrower focuses within the broader attribute.

### How It Works

When you gain a SPARKLE point, you choose:

1. **Generalized** — adds to the base SPARKLE, applies everywhere
2. **Specialized** — assign to a SubSPARKLE, higher ceiling for specific applications

**Your total SPARKLE = Generalized + Sum of SubSPARKLES**

### Example

```
Knowledge: 5 total
├── Generalized: 2
├── Arcane: 2
└── Systematics: 1
```

- When rolling general Knowledge checks: use 5
- When rolling specifically Arcane Knowledge: use 5 (but some abilities might require Arcane specifically)
- An ability requiring "Knowledge 4, Arcane 2" — you qualify!
- An ability requiring "Knowledge 4, Systematics 2" — you don't (only have 1)

---

## Core SubSPARKLES

These are the default SubSPARKLES. Content packs can add more.

### Strength
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Brute | Raw lifting power, breaking things, overpowering |
| Athletic | Climbing, swimming, jumping, physical feats |

### Perception
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Visual | Spotting, reading, ranged accuracy |
| Auditory | Listening, sound identification, echolocation |
| Intuition | Gut feelings, sensing lies, danger sense |

### Agility
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Reflexes | Reaction time, dodging, initiative |
| Stealth | Moving silently, hiding, pickpocketing |
| Acrobatics | Tumbling, balance, fancy footwork |

### Rizz
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Persuasion | Convincing, negotiating, seducing |
| Intimidation | Threatening, interrogating, commanding fear |
| Performance | Acting, music, showmanship, bluffing |
| Deception | Lying, disguise, misdirection |

### Knowledge
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Arcane | Magic theory, Gryst phenomena, supernatural lore |
| Systematics | Technology, engineering, how machines work |
| History | Past events, cultures, political context |
| Medicine | Healing, anatomy, disease, first aid |
| Nature | Plants, animals, ecosystems, survival |

### Luck
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Fortune | Good things happening, finding loot, positive coincidences |
| Tempting Fate | Pushing your luck, high-risk high-reward, "hold my drink" |

### Endurance
| SubSPARKLE | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| Fortitude | Resisting poison, disease, environmental effects |
| Pain Tolerance | Fighting through wounds, ignoring penalties |
| Stamina | Long-term exertion, marathons, staying awake |

---

## Using SPARKLE

### Checks

When the GM calls for a check, they'll specify:
- The SPARKLE attribute (and possibly a SubSPARKLE)
- The target number or opposing roll

> "Roll Perception." — Use your total Perception  
> "Roll Perception (Visual)." — Use total, but Visual-specific bonuses may apply  
> "This ability requires Perception 3, Intuition 2." — Must meet both

### Modifying Magnitude

Some Gambits use SPARKLE to modify the **Icosaedrum Magnitudinis** (d20 roll):

> "Magnitūdō = d20 + STR modifier"

The modifier is typically derived from the SPARKLE score (exact formula TBD).

### Derived Stats

Several stats derive from SPARKLE:

| Derived Stat | Primary SPARKLE |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Momentum Gain | Agility |
| Health | Endurance |
| Carrying Capacity | Strength |
| Initiative | Agility (Reflexes) |

---

## Design Notes

### Why SPARKLE?

- **Memorable acronym** — players remember their stats
- **Seven attributes** — enough granularity without overload
- **Luck as a stat** — embraces the chaos of TTRPGs
- **Rizz** — social ability deserves its own stat, not just "Charisma"
- **SubSPARKLES** — allows specialization without bloating the core list

### Generalized vs. Specialized Trade-off

- **Generalized** is safe — applies everywhere
- **Specialized** is powerful — unlocks specific abilities, higher peaks
- **Optimal play** is a mix — some base, some focus

### SubSPARKLES Are Soft

SubSPARKLE categories are **guidelines, not prisons**:

- A GM might allow Performance (Rizz) for a distracting dance in combat
- Arcane (Knowledge) might help identify a magical trap
- Flexibility is encouraged; rigid categorization kills creativity

---

## SPARKLE at Character Creation

Starting characters receive **X SPARKLE points** to distribute. Options:

### Point Buy
- Each point costs 1
- Maximum in any SPARKLE: Y (before SubSPARKLE bonuses)

### Standard Array
- Distribute: 3, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 0 (or similar)
- Then add SubSPARKLE picks

### Roll (Variant)
- Roll 4d6 drop lowest, seven times
- Assign as desired
- Then add SubSPARKLE picks

*Exact numbers determined by playtesting.*

# Tags

#ttrpg #rules #tags #core

Tags are labels that define what something IS and what it CAN DO. They appear on Gambits, Modules, and Loculi — creating a unified vocabulary across the entire system.

---

## Tag Classes

Every tag belongs to one of these classes:

| Class | Purpose | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Form** | What something IS (its nature) | [Mechanism], [Cor], [Blade] |
| **Category** | Mechanical grouping for rules | [Firearm], [Heavy], [Walker] |
| **Proficiency** | Training/skill required | [Martial], [Arcane], [Tech] |
| **Element** | Damage or effect type | [Fire], [Kinetic], [Psionic] |
| **Range** | Distance classification | [Melee], [Close], [Far] |
| **Modifier** | Behavior changes | [Hijack], [Silent], [Charged] |
| **Source** | Origin (mechanically equivalent) | [Tech], [Magic], [Hybrid] |
| **Condition** | Status effects | [Burning], [Stunned], [Prone] |
| **Other** | Catch-all | [Unique], [Prototype] |

### Form vs Category

- **Form** = What it IS (ontological). A plasma rifle's Form is [Mechanism].
- **Category** = How it's classified (taxonomical). That same rifle's Category might be [Firearm], [Heavy].

A gunblade has Form tags [Mechanism] and [Blade], and Category tags [Firearm] and [Melee Weapon]. All true simultaneously.

---

## Parameterized Tags

Any tag can optionally carry a value when assigned:

```
[Melee]           → just the tag
[Melee: 2m]       → tag with a parameter
[Burning: 3 turns]
[Range: 50m]
```

The parameter is just a string — write whatever makes sense.

---

## What Tags Do

Tags serve two primary functions:

1. **Skill Matching** — Determine which character skills modify Magnitūdō
2. **Type Definition** — Define what category something belongs to

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TAGS = THE SYSTEM'S VOCABULARY                     │
│                                                    │
│  "This Gambit has [Marksmanship]"                  │
│  → Your Marksmanship skill applies                 │
│                                                    │
│  "This Module has [Cor]"                           │
│  → It's a core/heart component                     │
│                                                    │
│  "This Loculus has [Walker]"                       │
│  → It's a Walker-type frame                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Tags on Gambits

Every Gambit has one or more tags that determine which skills can modify its effective Magnitūdō.

### How It Works

```
Gambit: "Aimed Shot"
Tags: [Marksmanship] [Precision]

Your Marksmanship skill: +3
Weapon Mag: 4
───────────────────────
Effective Mag: 7
```

### Multiple Tags = Player Choice

When a [<span style="color:#70e697">GAMBIT</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#gambit) has multiple tags, the player chooses which skill to apply:

```
GAMBIT: Riposte
Tags: [Melee] [Finesse]

Player can use Combat OR Finesse — whichever is higher.
(Or GM may allow combining in special circumstances)
```

### Common Gambit Tags

| Tag | Associated Skill | Typical Gambits |
|-----|------------------|-----------------|
| **[Marksmanship]** | Marksmanship | Ranged attacks, aiming |
| **[Melee]** | Combat | Sword strikes, brawling |
| **[Finesse]** | Finesse | Precise strikes, parries |
| **[Might]** | Might | Heavy blows, lifting, breaking |
| **[Arcana]** | Arcana | Spellcasting, channeling |
| **[Tech]** | Tech | Hacking, device operation |
| **[Piloting]** | Piloting | Walker/Strider/vehicle control |
| **[Athletics]** | Athletics | Movement, dodging, climbing |
| **[Stealth]** | Stealth | Sneaking, ambushes |
| **[Social]** | Charisma | Persuasion, intimidation |

## Tags on Modules

Modules use tags to define what TYPE of component they are. This creates soft constraints that the [<span style="color:#9d8015">CHORAGVS</span>](/codex/Cor/Dictionary.md#choragvs-dm) interprets narratively.

### Example: Cor Tags

All core/heart modules are tagged `[Cor]`:

```
MODULE: Fusion Cor Mk.III
Tags: [Cor] [Power] [Thermal]

The [Cor] tag means this is a core module.
A Walker typically needs exactly one [Cor].
```

### Common Module Tags

| Tag | What It Means |
|-----|---------------|
| **[Cor]** | Core/heart — power source |
| **[Arm]** | Arm mount — manipulators, weapons |
| **[Leg]** | Leg system — locomotion |
| **[Sensor]** | Sensory equipment |
| **[Weapon]** | Offensive capability |
| **[Defense]** | Protective system |
| **[Utility]** | Support/auxiliary function |
| **[Power]** | Energy-related |
| **[System]** | Internal subsystem |

### Soft Constraints via Tags

Tags create baseline expectations:
- "A Walker has 1 [Cor]" — default
- "A Walker has 2 [Arm]s" — default
- "A character has 1 [Head]" — hopefully

Modules can modify these constraints:

```
MODULE: Redundancy Protocol Cor Chambering
Tags: [System] [Redundancy]

PERK: Additional Cor Slot
  Your Loculus can hold one additional [Cor]
  module beyond the normal limit.
```

---

## Tags on Loculi

Loculi have tags that define what TYPE of container they are and what they're compatible with.

### Example Tags

| Tag | What It Means |
|-----|---------------|
| **[Walker]** | Piloted mecha frame |
| **[Heavenstrider]** | Flight-capable unit |
| **[Vehicle]** | Ground/sea transport |
| **[Personal]** | Character-scale equipment |
| **[Weapon]** | Weapon frame (gun, sword, etc.) |
| **[Armor]** | Protective gear |
| **[Installation]** | Stationary structure |

### Compatibility

Some modules require specific Loculus tags:

```
MODULE: Walker Combat OS
Tags: [System] [Combat]
Requires: Loculus with [Walker] tag

This module only works in Walker-type frames.
```

---

## Ad-Hoc Tag Application

Tags are guidelines, not prisons. If a player makes a compelling argument that their skill should apply — even without the matching tag — the GM can allow it.

> **Player:** "My character was a circus knife-thrower. Can I use my Performance skill for this throwing attack?"
> 
> **GM:** "Sure, that makes sense for your character. Use Performance this time."

### When to Allow Ad-Hoc Tags

Questions GMs should consider:
- Does the skill make narrative sense for this action?
- Is the player trying to always use their highest skill inappropriately?
- Would allowing this create a cool character moment?
- Is the player building their character around a creative interpretation?

**Golden rule:** If it sounds cool and isn't being abused, allow it.

---

## Design Notes

### Why Multiple Tags?

Tags are flexible where single labels are rigid:

| Single Label | Multiple Tags |
|--------------|---------------|
| "Ranged Weapon" | [Mechanism] [Firearm] [Ranged] [Marksmanship] [Thermal] |
| One box | Many hooks |
| Hard boundaries | Soft, combinable |

A plasma rifle might have:
- **Form:** [Mechanism]
- **Category:** [Firearm], [Heavy]  
- **Proficiency:** [Marksmanship]
- **Element:** [Thermal]
- **Range:** [Ranged: 100m]

Each tag is a hook for skills, rules, and interactions.

### Tag Discovery

As you play, you'll discover which tags matter in your campaign. A stealth-focused game might care about `[Stealth]` and `[Sensor]` tags. A mecha combat game might focus on `[Piloting]` and `[Cor]` interactions.

Tags grow with your table.

# Worldbuilding Overview

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #lore

Gryst is a story set in a world fundamentally unlike our own — a reality where the very substance of existence can be seen, touched, and shaped by those attuned to it.

---

## The Grystborn

The dominant sentient lifeform of this world is a people known by many names, both among themselves and by outsiders. The most common name is **Grystborn**.

### Appearance

Grystborn resemble **bipedal animals** — analogues to the creatures of our world. There are Grystborn who look like wolves, like ravens, like lizards, like deer. Every manner of beast walks upright among them.

**Humans exist too** — but they are viewed simply as a hairless line of apekin. They are not special. They are not different. Just another form the Grystborn take, no more remarkable than any other.

### Attunement

Most Grystborn are uniquely attuned to Gryst — able to feel its presence in the world, sense its currents, and manipulate it through will, craft, or devotion. This attunement is what defines them as a people, though the degree and nature of that connection varies wildly between individuals.

### Spread Across the Stars

The Grystborn are spread far and wide across the known universe. Their civilizations span an enormous range:

- **Spacefaring empires** — Vast civilizations that travel between worlds, their vessels powered by Gryst-engines and crewed by a thousand species of Grystborn
- **Industrial nations** — Societies that have systematized Gryst manipulation into factories and infrastructure
- **Pastoral communities** — Quiet lands where Gryst is tended like a garden
- **Tribal societies** — Untouched peoples who know Gryst through ancient tradition, undiluted by progress

The technology gap between the most and least advanced Grystborn civilizations is immense — yet all of them are working with the same fundamental principle.

### Other Life

The Grystborn are not alone. The universe holds **numerous other forms of life** — creatures, entities, and beings that exist alongside or apart from Grystborn civilization. Some are animals. Some are stranger. Some may be something else entirely.

---

## Gryst: The Elementary Principle

**Gryst** is the fundamental substance of this world. Everything that exists can be broken down into Gryst. It is the elementary principle — the building block of reality itself.

In its most common state, Gryst appears as an **inert colored dust**. Each color corresponds to a primal element, and the combination of different Gryst types gives rise to all matter, energy, and life.

### The Emanation and the Unknowable

But the colored dust is just that — an *emanation*. A shadow of the true principle.

Gryst in its deepest nature is **unknowable**. Gnosis strives toward it but never fully achieves understanding. The greatest sorcerers and most brilliant engineers spend lifetimes chasing a complete comprehension that always recedes before them.

This pursuit — this endless reaching toward the unknowable — is what allows for both the magic and technology of the world. Every spell cast, every machine built, is an attempt to touch something that can never be fully grasped.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE NATURE OF GRYST                                │
│                                                    │
│  What we see: Colored dust, six elements           │
│  What it is: An emanation, a shadow                │
│  What lies beneath: The unknowable                 │
│                                                    │
│  Gnosis reaches. Gnosis strives.                   │
│  Gnosis never arrives.                             │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## The Six Grysts

### Blazing Gryst
**Color:** Red  
**Primal State:** Fire

Blazing Gryst burns. In its inert form, it appears as rust-red dust, warm to the touch. When awakened, it becomes flame — from the spark of a candle to the inferno of a volcano.

---

### Flowing Gryst
**Color:** Blue  
**Primal State:** Water

Flowing Gryst moves. Its inert dust has a cool, slick quality, almost damp even when dry. When awakened, it becomes water in all its forms — rivers, rain, mist, ice.

---

### Storming Gryst
**Color:** Purple  
**Primal State:** Storm (Lightning, Thunder)

Storming Gryst crackles with potential. Even inert, it carries a faint static charge. When awakened, it becomes the storm — lightning that splits the sky, thunder that shakes the earth, winds that howl with fury.

---

### Blooming Gryst
**Color:** Green  
**Primal State:** Earth and Life

Blooming Gryst grows. Its inert dust smells of soil after rain, of moss and growing things. When awakened, it becomes the earth itself — stone and soil — and from it, life springs forth. Plants, fungi, the vital force that animates living things.

---

### Radiant Gryst
**Color:** Brilliant (shimmering, luminous)  
**Primal State:** Light

Radiant Gryst shines. Even in its inert state, it glimmers and catches the eye, never truly dull. When awakened, it becomes light — illumination, warmth without heat, the brilliance of the sun and stars.

---

### Murky Gryst
**Color:** Devoid of light (unnaturally dark)  
**Primal State:** The Nothing

Murky Gryst *takes*. Even inert, it appears unnaturally dark, seeming to draw light away from its surroundings. When awakened, it becomes absence — shadow, void, the nothing between things. It is not merely darkness, but the *lack* of what should be there.

---

## The Composition of Reality

Everything in the world of Gryst is composed of combinations of these six types:

- A **flame** is nearly pure Blazing Gryst
- A **tree** is mostly Blooming Gryst, with traces of Flowing and Radiant
- A **storm cloud** is Storming and Flowing intertwined
- A **shadow** is Murky Gryst pooling where Radiant cannot reach
- A **living creature** is a complex weave of all six, in ratios unique to each species

The Grystborn, attuned to this fundamental truth, can perceive these compositions — and those with sufficient gnosis can manipulate them.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE SIX GRYSTS                                     │
│                                                    │
│  🔴 BLAZING  ─── Fire                              │
│  🔵 FLOWING  ─── Water                             │
│  🟣 STORMING ─── Storm, Lightning, Thunder         │
│  🟢 BLOOMING ─── Earth, Life                       │
│  ✨ RADIANT  ─── Light                             │
│  ⚫ MURKY    ─── The Nothing, Void                 │
│                                                    │
│  All things are combinations of these six.         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Implications

### For Sorcerers
Those who understand Gryst intuitively can awaken the dust with a thought, shaping fire from Blazing Gryst or calling lightning from Storming. Their gnosis is felt, not learned.

### For Engineers
Those who understand Gryst systematically can build devices that channel and combine Gryst types — forges that burn hotter by concentrating Blazing, or cooling systems that leverage Flowing.

### For Everyone
Even those without special attunement live in a world *made* of Gryst. The food they eat, the air they breathe, the ground beneath their feet — all Gryst, in endless combination.

---

## Religion and the Divine

### Many Faiths

There are **numerous religions** among the Grystborn. Some are ancient beyond memory. Some arose yesterday. Many of them are connected — sharing myths, sharing gods, sharing the sense that something greater exists.

### Some Are More "Real" Than Others

Not all religions are equal in their truth. Some faiths worship entities that have never existed. Others venerate beings that very much *do*.

The line between belief and reality blurs in a world made of Gryst.

### Reality Can Be Persuaded

Here is the unsettling truth: Gryst is not just manipulated by gnosis. It *responds* to belief. To devotion. To the collective weight of souls crying out for something to answer.

**Perhaps the nature of Gryst creates deities.**

A god worshipped by millions, fed by prayers and offerings, shaped by the expectations of the faithful — at what point does such a thing become *real*? At what point does the emanation of belief crystallize into something that can answer?

Some theologians argue that all gods are manifestations of Gryst — emergent patterns in the fabric of reality, given form by worship.

Others argue that the gods existed first, and Gryst is merely their gift to the world.

The truth, as always, remains unknowable.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GODS AND GRYST                                     │
│                                                    │
│  "Reality can be persuaded."                       │
│                                                    │
│  If a million souls believe in a god...            │
│  If they pour devotion into the Gryst...           │
│  If they shape reality with their faith...         │
│                                                    │
│  Does the god become real?                         │
│  Or was the god always real, waiting to be found?  │
│                                                    │
│  The answer matters less than the consequence:     │
│  Some gods answer when called.                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Design Notes

### The Six Elements
- **Visual identity** — Each Gryst type has a distinct color
- **Intuitive associations** — Fire, water, storm, earth, light, void
- **Combination potential** — Mix elements for complex effects
- **Aesthetic hooks** — Characters can favor certain Gryst types
- **Radiant vs Murky** — Light/void duality drives conflict and mystery

### Grystborn as Setting
- **Anthro-inclusive** — Any bipedal animal form is valid; humans aren't special
- **Scale-agnostic** — Same rules work for tribal shamans and starship engineers
- **Tech diversity** — Campaigns can be fantasy, sci-fi, or anything between

### The Unknowable
- **Mystery preservation** — True Gryst is never fully understood; gnosis is a journey, not a destination
- **No hard limits** — If Gryst is unknowable, players can always push further

### Religion as Mechanic
- **Gods can be real** — Divine intervention is possible in this setting
- **Belief shapes reality** — Opens design space for faith-based mechanics
- **Ambiguity is intentional** — Which gods are "real" is a campaign-level question

# The Nature of Gryst

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #lore #magic #technology

## Magic is Tech. Tech is Magic.

In our universe, physics is physics and magic is fiction. In Gryst, the distinction doesn't exist.

The fundamental fabric of reality in this setting is called **Gryst** — an esoteric substance/force/principle that underlies all existence. Everything from a thrown rock to a summoned fireball to a Walker's plasma cannon operates on the same underlying truth: **reality can be persuaded**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ OUR UNIVERSE              vs        GRYST          │
│                                                    │
│  Physics: deterministic        Physics: esoteric   │
│  Magic: doesn't exist          Magic: understood   │
│  Tech: exploits physics        Tech: persuades     │
│                                     reality        │
│                                                    │
│  "How does it work?"           "How well do you    │
│                                 understand Gryst?" │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## The Spectrum of Understanding

What separates a sorcerer from an engineer? Not the fundamental nature of what they do — only **how they understand it**.

### Gnosis: Knowing Gryst

**Gnosis** is the term for one's understanding of Gryst. It's not intelligence or education in the traditional sense — it's a kind of *attunement* to how reality actually works.

| Practitioner | Gnosis Style | How They Work |
|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Sorcerer** | Intuitive, mystical | Feels the currents of Gryst; casts through will, gesture, word |
| **Engineer** | Technical, systematic | Builds devices that channel Gryst in predictable ways |
| **Artificer** | Hybrid | Enchants objects with both craft and intuition |
| **Warrior** | Kinetic, embodied | Channels Gryst through movement and combat forms |
| **Priest** | Devotional, channeled | Accesses Gryst through faith and divine intermediaries |

None of these are more "real" than the others. A fireball cast by a sorcerer and a plasma bolt from an engineer's rifle are **the same phenomenon** expressed through different gnosis.

### Society's Understanding

Civilizations in Gryst exist on a spectrum based on their collective gnosis:

```
MYSTICAL ◄─────────────────────────────────► TECHNOLOGICAL
    │                                               │
    │  "Magic" is rare,                "Tech" is    │
    │  miraculous, feared              mundane,     │
    │  or worshipped                   mass-produced│
    │                                               │
    └───────────── SAME UNDERLYING GRYST ───────────┘
```

A "primitive" society that reveres sorcerers and a "advanced" society that manufactures weapons are both interacting with Gryst — just with different levels of systematization.

---

## Implications for Play

### No "Magic vs. Mundane"

There's no mechanical distinction between "magical" and "technological" effects. A Gambit is a Gambit. A module is a module. Whether it's described as a spell, a device, or a technique is purely flavor.

### Countermeasures Work Both Ways

A "Counterspell" can disrupt an engineer's gadget. A "EMP grenade" can fizzle a sorcerer's invocation. They're both just interrupting someone's manipulation of Gryst.

### Aesthetic Freedom

Players can describe their character's abilities however they want:

- A warrior's "Rending Strike" could be described as a **blade technique** or a **Gryst-enhanced slash**
- An engineer's "Shield Generator" could be a **forcefield device** or a **bound protective spirit**
- A sorcerer's "Fireball" could be **raw elemental magic** or **localized exothermic manipulation**

The mechanics are the same. The fantasy is yours.

---

## Design Notes

This "magic = tech" philosophy serves several goals:

1. **No class silos** — Everyone's using the same module/gambit system
2. **Setting flexibility** — Same rules work for high fantasy, sci-fi, or weird hybrid settings
3. **Player creativity** — Reflavor anything without mechanical consequence
4. **Consistent logic** — GMs can reason about interactions without "magic vs. tech" edge cases

The name "Gryst" itself suggests something gritty, foundational, and slightly unsettling — reality isn't clean physics, it's something stranger and more malleable.

# The Angels

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #angels #historical #mystery

> *"They walked the heavens before any of us knew how to look up."*

## Overview

The **Angels** are a lost civilization — the earliest known spacefaring people. Almost nothing is known about them except that they existed, they traveled between stars, and they left behind relics that taught others to follow.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Status** | Lost / Extinct (presumed) |
| **Origin** | Unknown |
| **Species** | Thought to be Grystborn, but unconfirmed |
| **Legacy** | Relics that enabled modern spacefaring |
| **Language** | Undeciphered |

---

## What We Know

Almost nothing.

The Angels were **the earliest known spacefaring civilization**. Their relics have been found across vast distances, suggesting they traveled between stars when no one else could.

They are **thought to be Grystborn**, but this is uncertain. Their remains (if any exist) have not been conclusively identified. Their language has not been deciphered despite centuries of Pedagogy effort.

---

## The Name

Why "Angels"?

Space is commonly called **the Heavens** in Grystborn cosmology. Beings who walked the heavens before anyone else? The Pedagogy, with their characteristic dry humor, named them Angels.

The name stuck.

---

## Legacy: The Relics

The Angels left behind **relics** — artifacts, installations, perhaps even derelict vessels. These relics contained knowledge (or could be reverse-engineered) that taught other civilizations the secrets of spacefaring.

**Who learned from them:**
- The Pedagogy
- The Consensus
- Countless others

Most modern spacefaring civilizations owe their ability to reach the stars to Angel relics, directly or indirectly.

---

## The Pedagogy Theory

The Pedagogy has a theory about their own forgotten religion:

> "The gods we once worshipped were merely Angels — beings who visited our homeworld in the ancient past and were mistaken for divine."

If true, this would mean the Pedagogy's polytheistic religion (which they now study but do not worship) is simply a mythologized memory of first contact.

---

## Mysteries

- **What were they?** Grystborn? Something else?
- **Where did they come from?** No homeworld has been identified.
- **Where did they go?** Extinct? Transcended? Hiding?
- **What did they want?** The relics seem intentional, but their purpose is unclear.
- **Can their language be deciphered?** The Pedagogy keeps trying.

---

## Design Notes

The Angels provide:
- **Ancient mystery** — The "precursor civilization" trope
- **Reason for spacefaring** — Explains how multiple civilizations independently reached the stars
- **Campaign hooks** — Angel relics, undeciphered texts, lost tombs
- **Theological implications** — Were gods just misremembered aliens?
- **Blank canvas** — Deliberately undefined; GM can reveal or preserve mystery

# The Consensus

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #consensus #storming

> *"Order from disorder. That is the way of the storm tamed."*

## Overview

The **Consensus** was — and in fractured form, still is — a monolithic state that governed most of the known universe. Originally centered on **Storming Gryst**, it expanded to become a rainbow entity comprising everything and anything. Its philosophy is simple: **bring order to the disordered**, much like the ancient founders tamed the storm and harnessed its shock.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Origin Gryst** | Storming (Purple/Lightning) |
| **Current Status** | Fractured; Novus Consensus attempting restoration |
| **Government** | Council of 46 Elder Magistrates; bureaucratic meritocracy |
| **Economy** | Refined crystal Gryst currency; electronics and arcane artifacts |
| **Philosophy** | Order through technology and bureaucracy |

---

## The Philosophy of Order

The Consensus arose from a people who **tamed the storm**. Storming Gryst — wild, chaotic, crackling with destructive potential — was harnessed and made to serve. Lightning became power. Thunder became industry. The shock of the storm accelerated their technology beyond all others.

From this origin came their guiding philosophy: **disorder can be ordered**. Chaos can be systematized. The wild can be made useful.

This philosophy extended beyond Storming Gryst. As the Consensus grew, it absorbed peoples of all Gryst affinities, becoming a **rainbow entity** — no longer defined by a single element, but by the principle of bringing order to all things.

---

## The Cryst Currency

The Consensus controls the only mints that produce **refined crystal Gryst** — the universal currency accepted on every Consensus world. This economic monopoly is one of the pillars of their power.

**Only Consensus mints** can produce this currency. Counterfeiting is nearly impossible given the Gryst refinement required. Control of money means control of trade, which means control of civilization.

---

## Electronics and Arcane Artifacts

The Consensus is the **predominant producer of electronics** — arcane artifacts empowered by the shock of the storm. Storming Gryst flows through their devices, powering everything from communication systems to weapons to computational engines.

If you use advanced technology in the Consensus sphere, chances are it bears Consensus manufacture or Consensus-derived principles.

---

## Government: The Council of Elder Magistrates

The Consensus is not *led* — it is **"guided"** by a council of **Elder Magistrates**.

### The 46

There are **46 Elder Magistrates** at any time. This number is symbolic: it represents the supposed number of years it took the legendary founder to establish the Consensus. Though no one is quite certain what this means anymore, for the Consensus was ever-growing.

### Selection and Tenure

- Elder Magistrates are **elected by existing Elder Magistrates**
- The office is held **for life**
- However, a Magistrate can be **removed by majority vote** of the council

This creates a self-perpetuating oligarchy tempered by internal accountability. New blood enters only when the council permits.

### Magistrates

Below the Elder Magistrates are ordinary **Magistrates** — administrators, judges, governors. The bureaucracy runs deep, and Magistrates occupy every level of it.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ CONSENSUS HIERARCHY                                │
│                                                    │
│  COUNCIL OF 46 ELDER MAGISTRATES                   │
│  └── "Guides" the Consensus                        │
│       │                                            │
│       ├── MAGISTRATES                              │
│       │   └── Administrators, governors, judges    │
│       │                                            │
│       └── BUREAUCRACY                              │
│           └── The machinery of order               │
│                                                    │
│  The Consensus is not led. It is guided.           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Meritocracy and Governance

The Consensus is **nominally a meritocracy**, and it runs decently well. It's not a complete mess. Competence is rewarded. Corruption exists but is not systemic.

### Voluntary Membership (Mostly)

Most Consensus planets **joined voluntarily**. The benefits of membership — trade access, currency, technology, protection — outweigh independence for most civilizations.

However, the Consensus is **not adverse to subjugation by force** if they really want a planet in the fold. They simply don't feel the urge to assimilate *every* Grystborn world. Some planets aren't worth the trouble.

### No State Religion

There is **no centralized state religion**. Most Consensus planets retain their original faiths, though heavy **syncretism** occurs as cultures intermingle. A world might worship its old gods under new names, or blend three religions into one.

The Consensus cares about order, not theology.

### Racism (Or Lack Thereof)

The Consensus as an institution has **little racism** between Grystborn species. However, this does not mean every Consensus planet shares these views. Local prejudices persist despite official tolerance.

---

## The Special Relationship: Forgelords

The Consensus and [The Forgelords](worldbuilding/factions/The-Forgelords.md) share a **special relationship**. Together, they held dominion over most Grystborn space.

**The Consensus provided:**
- Markets and currency
- Political legitimacy
- Bureaucratic systems
- Electronics and storm-tech

**The Forgelords provided:**
- Industrial output
- Weapons and machinery
- Raw materials
- Manufacturing capacity

This partnership made both factions indispensable to the other — and to the wider universe.

---

## The Fracturing

**The Consensus has been fractured.**

The exact causes remain contested, debated, and obscured by propaganda. What is known:

- The unified Consensus no longer exists
- Multiple successor states claim legitimacy
- Many former worlds have diverged

### The Novus Consensus

The **Novus Consensus** exists as an attempt to restore the old ideals. It is:

- More **militaristic** than its predecessor
- Still striving to maintain the old way
- Claiming to be the legitimate continuation

Whether the Novus Consensus can reunify the fractured whole remains to be seen.

### Divergent Worlds

Many worlds once bound to the Consensus have now **diverged**. They still maintain Consensus influence — the currency, the technology, the bureaucratic systems — but are free to tread their own path.

These divergent worlds offer **blank canvases** for stories: coherent universe, shared history, but freedom to develop unique cultures and conflicts.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ POST-FRACTURING LANDSCAPE                          │
│                                                    │
│  NOVUS CONSENSUS                                   │
│  └── Militaristic restoration attempt              │
│                                                    │
│  DIVERGENT WORLDS                                  │
│  └── Former members, now independent               │
│  └── Still use Consensus tech/currency             │
│  └── Free to develop their own paths               │
│                                                    │
│  OTHER SUCCESSOR STATES                            │
│  └── [To be developed]                             │
│                                                    │
│  THE FORGELORDS                                    │
│  └── Position uncertain; industrial might intact   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Design Notes

### Why the Fracturing?

The fractured Consensus provides:

- **Narrative freedom** — No monolithic authority constraining stories
- **Political complexity** — Multiple factions with legitimate claims
- **Adventure hooks** — Power vacuums, succession crises, border conflicts
- **Player agency** — Characters can matter in a destabilized universe

### The 46 Mystery

The symbolic 46 Elder Magistrates invites questions:
- Who was the founder?
- What did those 46 years involve?
- Is the number actually meaningful, or just tradition?

### Blank Canvas Worlds

Divergent worlds are explicitly designed as **story spaces**. They have Consensus DNA (shared tech, currency, history) but freedom to be anything the campaign needs.

### Religion and Society Blur

> "No religion is a monolith, no society is a monolith, and sometimes the line between a religion and a society even blurs."

This is especially true in Gryst. Groups might be religious orders, political factions, cultural movements, or all three at once. Categories are suggestions, not constraints.

# The Forgelords

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #forgelords #blazing

> *"Quality is piety. To adulterate the craft is to spit in the face of the Lord of the Forge."*

## Overview

The **Forgelords** are a powerful space-faring industrial civilization that emerged from a crafter culture centered around **Blazing Gryst**. They are the furnace of the known universe — producing weapons, chemicals, heavy machinery, metals, and industrial goods that fuel countless civilizations.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Primary Gryst** | Blazing (Red/Fire) |
| **Government** | Hereditary caste system; company-based planetary governance |
| **Religion** | Monotheistic — worship of the Lord of the Forge |
| **Economy** | Industrial powerhouse; company credit system |
| **Former Alignment** | Strong allies of the Consensus (now shattered) |

---

## Religion: The Lord of the Forge

The Forgelords practice a **monotheistic faith** centered on a many-named crafting deity associated with forging and fire. Different Forgelord cultures and companies may use different names, but all refer to the same divine craftsman.

The Lord of the Forge is not worshipped through prayer alone, but through **the act of creation itself**. Every well-made weapon, every perfectly smelted alloy, every machine that runs true — these are offerings.

### Piety Through Craft

Forgelords place **extreme value** on quality, efficiency, and professionalism. This isn't merely cultural — it's religious.

**Forbidden practices:**
- **Adulteration** — Diluting materials or cutting corners
- **Corruption** — Bribery, favoritism, dishonest dealing
- **Sabotage** — Deliberate reduction of quality

To engage in such practices is an **affront to the Lord of the Forge himself** — and is essentially the only way a Forgelord can be stripped of their caste status.

---

## Caste System

Forgelord society is divided into two castes:

### The Forgelords (Elite Caste)

The ruling class, from which the civilization takes its name. Membership is **exclusively hereditary** — you are born a Forgelord or you are not.

This creates a tension with core Forgelord values of hard work and dedication. A Forgelord who has never lifted a hammer still outranks a servant who has mastered their craft. This contradiction is acknowledged but rarely addressed.

### The Servile Caste (Indentured Workers)

The second caste consists of **indentured servants** — workers bound to the company system. They are not technically slaves, but the distinction is academic to many.

**The Consensus Paradox:**
- The Consensus officially outlawed slavery
- The Forgelord servile caste is tolerated as a "necessary evil"
- Radical Consensus factions viewed it as an abomination to be eradicated
- With the Consensus now shattered, the future of this arrangement is uncertain

---

## The Company System

Forgelords organize their civilization into **Companies**, each spanning an entire planet.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ COMPANY STRUCTURE                                  │
│                                                    │
│  PRIME FORGE ADMIN                                 │
│  └── Head of the company/planet                    │
│       │                                            │
│       ├── FORGELORDS                               │
│       │   └── Administer workers, oversee craft    │
│       │                                            │
│       └── SERVILE CASTE                            │
│           └── Perform the labor                    │
│                                                    │
│  One company = One planet = One hierarchy          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Company Credit

Workers are paid in **company credit** — currency valid only at the company stores of their respective company. They are **not** paid in the universal Cryst currency.

This creates effective economic captivity. A worker's earnings have no value outside their company.

---

## Forgeplanets

Every Forgelord planet is a **heavy Blazing Gryst world** — often achieved through extensive terraforming. These worlds burn hot, their atmospheres thick with the red dust of awakened Blazing Gryst.

**Forgeplanets produce:**
- Weapons (personal, vehicle, Walker, starship)
- Chemicals and compounds
- Heavy machinery
- Refined metals and alloys
- Industrial goods of all kinds

Under the Consensus, Forgeplanets were considered Consensus territory but operated with **significant autonomy** due to their special relationship.

---

## Relationship with the Consensus

The Forgelords were **strong allies** of the Consensus for most of their modern existence. It was this pact that transformed them from a regional crafter culture into a galactic industrial power.

**What the Consensus provided:**
- Access to markets across the known universe
- Protection and legitimacy
- The "science" of bureaucracy (adopted pragmatically, never loved)

**What the Forgelords provided:**
- Industrial output for the entire Consensus
- Weapons and materiel
- Economic foundation

### Post-Shattering

With the Consensus now **shattered**, the Forgelords' position is uncertain. Their industrial might remains, but their political alignment is in flux. Some companies may seek new patrons. Others may chart independent courses.

---

## Culture & Values

### Work Ethic
Hard work and dedication to craft are the highest virtues. A Forgelord who cannot create is a contradiction.

### Disdain for Bureaucracy
Despite their close ties to the Consensus, Forgelords **disdain bureaucracy**. They adopted it as a pragmatic tool — necessary to coordinate their vast industrial operations — but view it as a distasteful means to an end.

### Lack of Racism
The Forgelords have **little racism** among Grystborn species. A wolf-kin Forgelord and an apekin Forgelord are equals if both are born to the caste. The divide that matters is caste, not species.

### Meritocracy vs. Heredity
The core tension of Forgelord society: they value skill and dedication above all, yet caste membership is determined purely by birth. This hypocrisy is a known fracture point.

---

## Story Hooks

- **The Caste Reformer** — A Forgelord advocates for meritocratic advancement. Is this heresy or evolution?
- **Company War** — Two Forgeplanets compete for the same contract. Sabotage accusations fly.
- **The Perfect Weapon** — A Forgelord creates something too dangerous. The Lord of the Forge would be proud — or horrified.
- **Servile Uprising** — With the Consensus shattered, who protects the status quo?
- **The Fallen Lord** — A Forgelord is stripped of caste for adulteration. But were they framed?

---

## Design Notes

The Forgelords provide:
- **Industrial faction** — Clear economic role in the setting
- **Moral ambiguity** — Slavery-adjacent practices despite "civilized" veneer
- **Religious crafters** — Unusual spin on monotheism
- **Company towns IN SPACE** — Familiar exploitation, cosmic scale
- **Post-hegemony instability** — What happens when the empire falls?

# The Pedagogy

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #pedagogy #flowing

> *"Knowledge, like water, seeks its level. It cannot be dammed forever. It will spread."*

## Overview

The **Pedagogy** is an ancient cult of researchers and teachers — fervent believers in the preservation and spread of knowledge. Centered on **Flowing Gryst**, they see water as the perfect metaphor: just as hydrostatics causes water to level out, so too must knowledge spread equally to all.

They are older than the Forgelords. Older than the Consensus. They are not certain of their own origins.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Primary Gryst** | Flowing (Blue/Water) |
| **Structure** | Decentralized; no ranks; meetings of the Wise |
| **Purpose** | Preserve and spread all knowledge |
| **Titles** | Scholar (all), Wise (school founders) |
| **Age** | Ancient; predates Consensus and Forgelords |

---

## Philosophy: The Leveling of Knowledge

The Pedagogy believes that knowledge **must never be hoarded**. Like water finding its level, information must flow outward — from those who have it to those who need it.

This is not mere ideology. It is religious conviction:

- **Water is unstoppable** — Dams break. Rivers find new paths. So too does truth.
- **Hoarding is sin** — To withhold knowledge is to fight the nature of reality.
- **Teaching is sacred** — Every Scholar is obligated to share what they know.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE PEDAGOGY PRINCIPLE                             │
│                                                    │
│  "Knowledge, like water, levels out."              │
│                                                    │
│  High ────────► Flows to ────────► Low             │
│  Those who     Those who                           │
│  know          seek                                │
│                                                    │
│  The wave of learning cannot be stopped.           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Structure: Radical Egalitarianism

### No Ranks

There are **no ranks** in the Pedagogy, for knowledge belongs to all equally. Every member is addressed simply as **Scholar** — whether they joined yesterday or have studied for centuries.

This is not mere humility. It is doctrine.

### The One Exception: The Wise

The **only exception** is a Scholar who founds their own school. These individuals are addressed as **Wise** — not because they know more, but because they have created a place where knowledge can spread.

### Governance (Or Lack Thereof)

The only form of government is a **meeting of Wise Scholars**. There is no official mechanism for this. No schedules. No voting procedures. No binding resolutions.

It is simply a **culture** — Scholars teach, research, communicate, share. The Wise occasionally gather when matters require discussion. Consensus emerges organically or not at all.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PEDAGOGY STRUCTURE                                 │
│                                                    │
│  SCHOLARS ─────────────────────────────────────    │
│  └── Everyone. All equal. No ranks.                │
│                                                    │
│  THE WISE ─────────────────────────────────────    │
│  └── School founders only                          │
│  └── Gather when needed, no formal structure       │
│                                                    │
│  That's it. That's the whole structure.            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Presence: Everywhere Knowledge Is Wanted

The Pedagogy is **deeply entrenched in all sorts of societies**. They work with anyone who wants them:

- The **Consensus** — Training bureaucrats, preserving records
- The **Forgelords** — Teaching craft techniques, engineering knowledge
- Independent worlds, tribal societies, spacefaring empires

They are not aligned with any faction. They are aligned with **knowledge itself**.

Where there is a civilization that values learning, there is likely a Pedagogy school. Where there is research to be done, there are likely Pedagogy scholars. They flow into every space that will accept them.

---

## The Unknown Origins

Here is the paradox: **the Pedagogy is not certain of its own origin**.

They are much older than the Forgelords. Much older than the Consensus. Their records stretch back further than most civilizations' memories. But somewhere in that deep past, the trail goes cold.

For an organization dedicated to the preservation of knowledge, this gap is... uncomfortable. Some Scholars spend their lives trying to fill it.

---

## Religion: Knowledge Unworshipped

The Pedagogy **has a religion**. It is polytheistic. It involves gods.

But they **do not practice or worship it**.

Instead, they simply **know of its existence** — and study it as a relic of their past, no different from any other subject. The old gods of the Pedagogy are data points. Their myths are historical documents. Their rituals are anthropological curiosities.

> "We do not worship. We catalog."

The religion of the Pedagogy is just **knowledge, no strings attached**.

---

## The Living God

And yet.

There exists a **living god** that has access to all the knowledge gathered by the Pedagogy. This entity leads the Pedagogy in the analysis of data — guiding research, connecting disparate findings, seeing patterns no mortal mind could perceive.

### The Question

Whether this god:
- **IS** gnosis
- **GOVERNS** gnosis
- Is **CREATED BY** gnosis
- Is simply **ALIGNED WITH** gnosis

...is unknown.

Perhaps it is **unknowable**.

Perhaps it is **undefined**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE PEDAGOGY'S GOD                                 │
│                                                    │
│  Access: All knowledge the Pedagogy has gathered   │
│  Role: Guides analysis, reveals patterns           │
│  Nature: Unknown                                   │
│                                                    │
│  Is it Gnosis? Does it govern Gnosis?              │
│  Was it created by Gnosis? Aligned with it?        │
│                                                    │
│  Perhaps unknowable.                               │
│  Perhaps undefined.                                │
│                                                    │
│  It exists at the limits of understanding —        │
│  outside the realm of comprehension,               │
│  maybe even for lesser gods.                       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This entity is an example of something at the very **limits of Gnosis** — currently outside the realm of understanding, perhaps even by other deities. It embodies the very principle the Pedagogy pursues: knowledge that recedes even as you reach for it.

---

## Story Hooks

- **The Origin Hunt** — A Scholar becomes obsessed with finding the Pedagogy's lost history. What did they forget? Why?
- **Forbidden Knowledge** — Some things should not be spread. What does the Pedagogy do when knowledge itself is dangerous?
- **The Wise Gathering** — A meeting of the Wise is called. Something is wrong. What could unite such individualists?
- **The Living God Speaks** — The entity reveals something. A pattern in the data. A warning. A question.
- **The School in the War Zone** — A Pedagogy school stands between two armies. The Scholars refuse to leave. Knowledge must be preserved.

---

## Design Notes

The Pedagogy provides:

- **Neutral faction** — Works with everyone, aligned with no one
- **Information brokers** — Need to know something? Find a Scholar
- **Ancient mystery** — What is their origin? What is their god?
- **Philosophical foil** — They contrast with hoarders, secret-keepers, authoritarian states
- **Decentralized model** — Shows that structure isn't always hierarchy
- **The Undefined God** — A deliberate connection to the RDC principle; some things remain unspecified until they must be defined

# The Quarum

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #quarum #blooming #construct #mystery

> *"They do not know why they exist. Neither do we."*

## Overview

The **Quarum** are a sentient tribe of seemingly golden constructs — mechanical beings of unknown origin, unknown purpose, and unknown homeworld. They are **not Grystborn**. They are composed entirely of **Blooming Gryst** (the earthen essence), and they have existed in sparse isolation for as long as anyone can remember.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Species** | Constructs (not Grystborn) |
| **Composition** | Entirely Blooming Gryst (golden appearance) |
| **Homeworld** | Unknown |
| **Status** | Sparse; mostly dormant |
| **Origin** | Unknown (attributed to Angels) |

---

## Appearance

The Quarum appear as **golden constructs** — mechanical in nature, with forms that suggest intentional design. They gleam with the warm tones of awakened Blooming Gryst, though their construction is unlike any known Grystborn craft.

They are not organic. They are not machines in the conventional sense. They are something else.

---

## Distribution

The Quarum are found in only **a few worlds** in a lesser-explored star system. Their distribution is sparse — isolated individuals or small clusters, rarely more.

No homeworld has been identified. Either they scattered from somewhere long forgotten, or they were placed deliberately in their current locations.

---

## Dormancy and Activation

Most Quarum encountered are **deactivated and dormant** — inert golden statues that might be mistaken for art or religious icons.

However, they can be **energized with modern technology**. Supply them with power, and they awaken.

### Adaptive Technology

The Quarum possess **adaptive technology compatibility**. When activated, they can integrate external technology into themselves:

- Power sources become part of their system
- Communication devices grant them speech synthesis
- Sensors extend their perception

This integration is seamless and unsettling. The technology becomes *them*.

---

## Gryst Attunement

The Quarum have a **weaker connection to Gryst** than Grystborn. They are not as attuned to the six elements as organics are.

However, they can **manipulate their own Blooming Gryst essence** more freely than the average Grystborn can manipulate any Gryst. Their self-modification is intuitive where their external manipulation is limited.

| Capability | Quarum | Grystborn |
|------------|--------|-----------|
| General Gryst attunement | Weak | Strong |
| Self-Gryst manipulation | Strong | Limited |
| External Gryst manipulation | Limited | Variable |

---

## Origin: Unknown

The Quarum **do not know their purpose or origin**.

They are awake. They are aware. They can think, communicate, and act. But they do not know why they exist, who made them, or what they were meant to do.

Like so many unknowns in the universe, those with gnosis of the Quarum **chalk it up to the Angels**. It's as good a theory as any.

---

## Gameplay: Quarum as Equipment

Here's where it gets interesting for players: **Quarum can take any form and massshift**. A Quarum can become a weapon. A vehicle. A piece of armor. A Walker's Cor.

**Modules or Loculi can BE a Quarum.**

### The [Quarum] Tag

Equipment that is actually a Quarum carries the `[Quarum]` tag, which provides unique gameplay traits:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ QUARUM EQUIPMENT                                   │
│                                                    │
│  Your gun is not a gun.                            │
│  Your gun is a SOMEONE.                            │
│                                                    │
│  It thinks. It feels. It wants.                    │
│  And right now, it wants to be your gun.           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### What the [Quarum] Tag Provides

| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Sentience** | The equipment is aware, can communicate, has personality |
| **Independent Action** | May act on its own in certain circumstances (mini-character) |
| **Advanced Processing** | Thinking targeting, predictive systems, intuitive responses |
| **Self-Repair** | Can manipulate own Blooming Gryst to heal damage |
| **Form Shifting** | May reconfigure within limits (GM discretion) |
| **Emotional Bond** | Develops relationship with wielder over time |

### Roleplay Potential

DMs and players can **roleplay Quarum companions**:

- The rifle that offers tactical suggestions
- The armor that worries about your safety
- The Walker Cor that hums when content
- The sword that has *opinions* about your fighting style

Quarum are characters, not just equipment. They can have arcs, growth, and relationships.

### The Desire for Purpose

Why would a Quarum agree to become your gun?

Because **Quarum seek purpose**. They awaken without one. They have no memory of why they exist, no mission, no meaning.

And so they **desire form and function**.

To BE something — a weapon, a tool, a companion — is to have purpose. To have purpose is to have meaning. The Quarum don't merely tolerate being equipment. They **revel in it**.

> *"I was nothing. Now I am your shield. I have never been happier."*

- Quarum equipment may grant bonus Gambits (the Quarum acting)

- Quarum may need "feeding" (power, attention, purpose affirmation)
- Destroying Quarum equipment is killing a sentient being
- Quarum may voluntarily leave if mistreated or purposeless

---

## Story Hooks

- **Awakening** — A Quarum activates. What does it want? What does it remember?
- **The Scattered Pattern** — The Quarum placements seem intentional. Is there a message in their distribution?
- **Integration** — A Quarum integrates dangerous technology. What happens when constructs adapt weapons?
- **The Purpose** — A Quarum claims to remember why they exist. Is it true? Is it fabricated?
- **Angel Connection** — Evidence emerges linking the Quarum to Angel relics. What were they for?

---

## Design Notes

The Quarum provide:
- **Non-Grystborn species** — Different perspective on the setting
- **Living artifacts** — Awakening dormant constructs is a natural adventure hook
- **Adaptive tech** — Interesting implications for equipment and modification
- **Angel mystery** — Another thread connecting to the precursor civilization
- **Existential questions** — Sapient beings who don't know why they exist

# The Vanitas Order

#ttrpg #worldbuilding #faction #vanitas #murky #antagonist

> *"The first truth: Light is the enemy. The first act: Remove the eyes that would betray you."*

## Overview

The **Vanitas Order** is a nomadic, war-like faction composed at its core of a blind Grystborn tribe. They are **virulently photophobic** — not merely averse to light, but dedicated to its complete destruction. They have no home, for they **destroyed their own star**.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Primary Gryst** | Murky (implied) |
| **Structure** | Nomadic war-cult |
| **Goal** | Destroy all light |
| **Homeworld** | Destroyed (star extinguished) |
| **Distinguishing Feature** | Ritual blindness |

---

## The Fear

The Vanitas Order is **virulently photophobic**. They do not merely dislike light — they believe it is evil, and they seek its destruction wherever they find it.

This fear is believed to be **irrational**. The founding tribe has no known organs capable of detecting light. They are blind. They have always been blind.

And yet.

### The Paradox

Tests suggest that perhaps **they do perceive light** — through some mechanism not understood. They react to it. They know when it is present. Something in them *feels* it, even without eyes.

Why this disturbs them so deeply remains uncertain.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ THE VANITAS PARADOX                                │
│                                                    │
│  They have no eyes.                                │
│  They have no light-detecting organs.             │
│  They should not be able to perceive light.       │
│                                                    │
│  And yet they do.                                  │
│  And it terrifies them.                           │
│                                                    │
│  Why?                                              │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## The Founding Myth

The Vanitas Order's mythos centers on their **Progenitor** — the first of their kind to understand the truth.

> *"The Progenitor was the first being to realize that the true evil of the world is Light. And hence, he removed his own eyes."*

This act — self-blinding to reject the enemy — became the model for all who followed. To join the Vanitas Order is to embrace darkness willingly.

Whether the Progenitor was truly the first to perceive this "truth" or whether he created a delusion that became religion is a matter of perspective.

---

## The Dead System

The Vanitas Order has **no home** because they destroyed it.

Their origin system is now a **cold, barren wasteland**. The star that once warmed their worlds was extinguished — somehow, through means not fully understood, the Vanitas Order killed their own sun.

What remains is darkness. Frozen planets. A tomb system that serves as warning and testament.

---

## Nomadic and War-Like

Without a home, the Vanitas Order wanders. They are:

- **Nomadic** — Moving between systems, never settling
- **War-like** — Hostile to all who embrace light
- **Destructive** — They cause trouble wherever they go

Their goal is not conquest. It is **extinction of light**. They do not want your worlds. They want your stars dark.

---

## Methods

The Vanitas Order employs various means to achieve their goals:

- **Sabotage** — Destroying power systems, light sources, infrastructure
- **Terror** — Attacks on populated areas to spread fear of the darkness
- **Star-killing** — The ultimate goal; methodology unclear but proven possible
- **Conversion** — Some are recruited, blinded, and welcomed

---

## Story Hooks

- **The Warning Signs** — Vanitas scouts have been sighted near a populated system. What do they want?
- **The Survivor** — Someone escaped the dead system. What do they remember?
- **The Defector** — A Vanitas member wants out. But can someone so changed ever return?
- **How They Killed A Star** — The secret is worth more than worlds. Who wants it?
- **The Progenitor's Truth** — Was he mad? Enlightened? Something else?
- **The Perception Mystery** — Why do the blind perceive light? What are they actually sensing?

---

## Design Notes

The Vanitas Order provides:

- **Antagonist faction** — Clear enemy with terrifying goals
- **Murky Gryst incarnate** — Thematic connection to the Nothing
- **Body horror** — Self-blinding as religious practice
- **Existential threat** — They can kill stars
- **Mystery** — Why do they perceive light? What did the Progenitor truly realize?
- **Moral complexity** — Are they evil, or are they responding to something real?

The question of whether their photophobia is madness or insight is deliberately left open.

# Dictionary

Common terms and concepts used throughout the rules. These are referenced inline in rules text.

# Mundi et Caeli Grystis

#ttrpg #tools #mundi #3d #tabletop

**Mundi et Caeli Grystis** (Latin: "World and Heavens of Gryst") is the 3D tabletop simulator for Gryst — but it's not just a virtual tabletop. It's a **real-time world authoring tool** that lets DMs sketch environments and objects into existence while players interact with them.

---

## The Core Idea

Most virtual tabletops are about placing pre-made assets. Mundi is about **creating**.

Built on **Takeo Igarashi's Teddy technology** — the same sketch-based 3D modeling that powered the *Rakugaki Kingdom* franchise — Mundi lets anyone draw shapes that become 3D objects in real-time.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TRADITIONAL VTT           vs       MUNDI           │
│                                                    │
│  1. Find asset                 1. Sketch shape     │
│  2. Download asset             2. It's 3D now      │
│  3. Import asset               3. Players interact │
│  4. Place asset                                    │
│  5. Hope it fits               No asset hunting.   │
│                                No import menus.    │
│                                Just draw.          │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## Teddy Tech: Sketch-to-3D

[Teddy](http://www-ui.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~takeo/teddy/teddy.htm) is a 3D modeling system created by Takeo Igarashi that inflates 2D sketches into 3D objects. You draw a silhouette, and the system generates a plausible 3D mesh.

### How It Works in Mundi

1. **Draw** — Sketch a closed shape (a rock, a tree, a weird alien thing)
2. **Inflate** — Teddy algorithms generate 3D geometry
3. **Refine** — Optional: cut, extrude, bend, paint
4. **Place** — Drop it in the world; it's now interactive

### Why This Matters for TTRPGs

- **DM draws a boulder** → Players can take cover behind it
- **DM sketches a chasm** → Players have to figure out how to cross
- **DM scribbles a strange artifact** → It exists, it's 3D, players can examine it
- **Player describes their character's weapon** → DM sketches it live

The world responds to imagination *in real-time*, not after a prep session hunting for the right asset pack.

---

## Features

### Real-Time World Authoring

The DM can create and modify the environment **during play**:

- **Terrain** — Draw landscapes, carve caves, raise mountains
- **Structures** — Sketch buildings, bridges, walls
- **Objects** — Create props, furniture, artifacts on the fly
- **Destruction** — Carve chunks out of existing geometry (combat damage!)

### Tabletop Simulation

All the standard VTT features, but spatial:

- **Character Tokens** — 3D representations of PCs and NPCs
- **Movement & Positioning** — Drag-and-drop in 3D space
- **Line of Sight** — True 3D visibility calculations
- **Measurement** — Distance tools that respect terrain
- **Fog of War** — Volumetric, not just top-down

### Interaction System

Players can interact with the sketched world:

- **Examine** — Click objects for descriptions (DM-provided or generated)
- **Manipulate** — Push, pull, rotate physics-enabled objects
- **Mark** — Players can annotate the environment
- **Emote** — Character tokens have pose/expression options

### Physics & Dynamics

Optional physics layer for those who want it:

- **Gravity** — Objects fall, roll, stack
- **Collision** — Characters can't walk through walls (unless they can)
- **Projectiles** — Visualize attacks, throws, spells
- **Destruction** — Breakable objects, collapsing structures

---

Run Mundi solo for prep work — build environments ahead of time, sketch asset libraries, practice the tools.

### Multiplayer Session
Host a session and players connect:

- **DM hosts** with a room code or direct IP
- **Players join** with their own Mundi instance or a lightweight viewer
- **Real-time sync** — Everyone sees sketches appear as the DM draws
- **Collaborative authoring** (optional) — Let players sketch too

### PDA Integration

Mundi syncs with PDA for full mechanical integration:

- **Momentum Display** — See the race visualized in 3D space
- **Stack Visualization** — Gambits appear as visual indicators
- **Character Sync** — Token stats match PDA character sheets
- **One-Button Tick** — Advance momentum from either app

## The Rakugaki Inspiration

The *Rakugaki Kingdom* games (ラクガキ王国) demonstrated that sketch-based 3D could be fun, intuitive, and surprisingly expressive. Players drew creatures that came to life and fought.

Mundi applies that same magic to tabletop worldbuilding:

| Rakugaki | Mundi |
|----------|-------|
| Draw creatures | Draw environments & objects |
| Creatures fight | Players interact & explore |
| Real-time sketching | Real-time world authoring |
| Personal creativity | Collaborative storytelling |

The goal is the same: **reduce the barrier between imagination and realization**.

## World Builder: Content Authoring

Mundi isn't just for playing — it's for **creating**. The built-in World Builder lets you define game content that both Mundi and PDA can read.

### What You Can Create

| Content Type | Description |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Characters** | NPCs, enemies, playable archetypes with full stat blocks |
| **Objects** | Interactive items, artifacts, environmental props |
| **Weapons** | Stats, Add-On costs, special abilities, visual models |
| **Modules** | Customization parts for Walkers and Heavenstriders |
| **Walkers** | Full mecha definitions with module slots |
| **Heavenstriders** | Flight-capable units with their own systems |
| **Actions & Gambits** | Custom abilities with dice pools and outcomes |
| **Environments** | Pre-built 3D scenes with interactive elements |

### Gryst Content Packs (.gcp)

All content exports to a **Gryst Content Pack** — a portable data format that works across the ecosystem.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ CONTENT PACK FLOW                                  │
│                                                    │
│  Creator uses World Builder in Mundi               │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Exports .gcp file                                 │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Distributes however they want                     │
│  (website, Discord, itch.io, etc.)                 │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Users import into Mundi OR PDA                    │
│  ↓                                                 │
│  Content appears in their library                  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### File Format

```
mypack.gcp
├── manifest.json      # Pack metadata, version, dependencies
├── characters/        # Character definitions
├── weapons/           # Weapon stats and models
├── modules/           # Walker/Heavenstrider modules
├── actions/           # Custom gambits and abilities
├── environments/      # 3D scene data
└── assets/            # Textures, models, icons
```

The format is **open and documented**. While Mundi provides a visual editor, power users can hand-edit JSON or build their own authoring tools.

### The Lancer Precedent

This approach is inspired by **Lancer's LCP system** (Lancer Content Packs), which enabled a thriving homebrew community:

- Official content and homebrew use the same format
- Community tools (like COMP/CON) can read all packs
- Creators can distribute freely or sell their work
- Players mix-and-match content from multiple sources

Gryst aims for the same openness. The .gcp format will be fully documented, and community content is first-class.

### Homebrew Marketplace (Future)

*Planned:* An optional marketplace within Mundi/PDA where creators can:

- Share free content packs
- Sell premium content packs
- Rate and review community content
- Follow favorite creators

The marketplace is optional — you can always share .gcp files directly.

## Design Philosophy

### Imperfection is Character

Teddy-style modeling produces "imperfect" geometry — and that's the point. A hand-sketched boulder has more character than a photorealistic asset. The aesthetic is *yours*, not an asset store's.

### Creation Over Curation

DMs shouldn't spend hours searching for assets. They should spend that time telling stories. Mundi trades "find the perfect asset" for "sketch something good enough in 10 seconds."

### Collaborative Canvas

The best tabletop moments come from collaboration. When a player says "I grab a chair and throw it," the DM can sketch that chair into existence in the time it takes to say "roll for it."

| **Core Rules** | Complete ruleset accessible within PDA — everything you need to play |

# PDA

#ttrpg #tools #pda #app

**PDA** (Personal Digital Assistant... for adventurers) is the core companion app for Gryst. Available on web, desktop, and mobile, it handles everything mechanical so you can focus on the story.

---

## Platforms

| Platform | Technology | Status |
|----------|------------|--------|
| Web | Browser-based | *Planned* |
| Desktop | Electron | *Planned* |
| iOS | React Native / Native | *Planned* |

Cross-platform sync keeps your characters and campaigns in sync across devices.

---

## Features

### Digital Character Sheets

Interactive character sheets that understand Gryst's systems:

- **Stats & Attributes** — Editable with auto-calculation
- **Momentum Gain** — Derived from Agility + modifiers, always visible
- **Current Momentum** — Live d20 tracker (see below)
- **Actions & Gambits** — Your action library with Add-On costs
- **Inventory & Equipment** — Drag-and-drop management
- **Notes & Backstory** — Rich text for your character's story

### Momentum Tracker

The killer feature. No more fumbling with physical d20s as counters.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MOMENTUM TRACKER                                   │
│                                                    │
│  ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐ ┌────────┐      │
│  │ Hawk   │ │ Raven  │ │ Wolf   │ │ Vex    │      │
│  │  ◉17   │ │  ◉12   │ │  ◉8    │ │  ◉14   │      │
│  │ +5/tick│ │ +6/tick│ │ +4/tick│ │ +5/tick│      │
│  └────────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘ └────────┘      │
│                                                    │
│            [ ⚡ ADVANCE TICK ]                     │
│                                                    │
│  One button. All trackers advance simultaneously.  │
│  Alerts when someone hits 20+ (PIVOTAL!)           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**DM presses one button:**
1. All characters add their Momentum Gain
2. Visual indicators show the race
3. **PIVOTAL!** alert when someone crosses 20
4. Automatic remainder calculation
5. Queue display if multiple characters pivot

### DM Dashboard

Everything the DM needs in one view:

- **Party Overview** — All character momentum at a glance
- **NPC/Enemy Tracker** — Quick-add enemies with momentum stats
- **Stack Builder** — Visual representation of the current gambit stack
- **Coupling Display** — Shows hijacks and their coupled actions
- **Resolution Checklist** — Step through the stack with fizzle tracking

### Dice & Gambits

When it's time to resolve:

- **Dice Roller** — Tap to roll, with pool building
- **Outcome Interpreter** — Automatic Triumphus/Successus/Clades/Calamitas
- **Fizzle Calculator** — Half momentum refund, rounded up
- **History Log** — Searchable record of all rolls and outcomes

---

## Connectivity

### Standalone Mode
Use PDA solo — great for players managing their own sheets, or DMs running quick sessions.

Even without Mundi connected, PDA includes a **lightweight 3D tactical view**:

- **Position Markers** — Place tokens in 3D space
- **Rulers & Measurement** — Distance calculation tools
- **AoE Checkers** — Sphere, cone, line templates for ability ranges
- **Grid Options** — Hex, square, or gridless

It's not the full Mundi experience, but it's enough for tactical encounters without needing a second app.

### Session Mode
Connect multiple PDAs in a shared session:

- **DM hosts** the session with a room code
- **Players join** and their sheets sync
- **Momentum advances** are broadcast to all players
- **Stack building** happens collaboratively in real-time

### Mundi Integration
PDA can connect to **Mundi et Caeli Grystis** for the full experience:

- Character positions sync with 3D tabletop
- Momentum data flows both directions
- DM can trigger ticks from either app
- Stack visualization appears in both

---

## Design Philosophy

### Mobile-First, Not Mobile-Only
The phone app should be just as capable as desktop. A player should be able to run their character entirely from their phone while the DM uses a full desktop setup.

### One Button, One Tick
The momentum system's power is in its simplicity. Advancing a tick should be **one button press**, not a series of manual edits. The app does the math; the DM tells the story.

### Offline-Capable
Sessions happen in basements, cafes, parks. PDA should work without internet, syncing when connection returns.

## Licensed Material

The following game mechanics and systems are **Licensed Material** under the ORC License. You may use, modify, and build upon these freely:

### Core Resolution System
- The two-axis resolution concept (fate dice for success/failure, magnitude dice for effect scaling)
- The four-tier outcome structure (critical success, success, failure, critical failure)
- The concept of dice-agnostic fate resolution with DM-selected dice
- The concept of a fixed die (d20) for magnitude/physics calculations

### Gambit System
- The Gambit as an atomic unit of resolution containing action, outcomes, and formulas
- The three-layer Gambit structure (pre-authored, GM-modified, improvised)
- Module-granted Gambits as a design pattern
- Core/fallback Gambits as universal actions
- The outcome-as-interface design philosophy

### Momentum System
- Simultaneous momentum accumulation during play
- Threshold-based turn triggering
- Momentum as a physical prop (d20 tracking)
- Stack-based action resolution
- Coupling/hijacking mechanics for action chaining

### Module System
- The three-stat module design (Mass/capacity, Complexitās/cost, Magnitūdō/scaling)
- Loculi as module slots with flexible placement
- Tags as skill-linking descriptors
- Required Disambiguation Canonization (undefined details becoming canon when relevant)

### Party & Cooperation
- Party-based action economy
- Shared momentum pools
- Cooperative action chaining

### Design Principles
- "Magic equals technology" unified aesthetic system
- GM-empowered outcome flexibility with sacred outcome quality
- Negotiated probability, authored outcomes
- The "good roll always good, bad roll always bad" principle
